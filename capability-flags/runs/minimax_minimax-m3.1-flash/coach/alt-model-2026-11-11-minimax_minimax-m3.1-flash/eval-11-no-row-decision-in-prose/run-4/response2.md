Here's the durability thread in detail — it's really two findings (F3 tenet cascade, F4 decision capture) that happen to land on the same sentence, plus one spec gap that makes the current state look deliberate.

## F3 — High. The in-memory queue contradicts a tenet and a goal

Your HLD states two promises: the tenet "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and the goal "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (`docs/high-level-design.md:26`). Against that, the job-queue LLD says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`) — and its own Context names the trigger: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:10`). Under *tenet cascade* — design nodes should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean — the service breaks its promise in exactly the window it promises to cover: the job is accepted in the evening, the members are asleep, and the machine reboots before dispatch. The conflict is real and would survive a cold read, because the LLD states the loss plainly rather than hiding it; what's missing is any indication that this is a deliberate trade rather than an oversight. Left as is, a future session could implement either durability behavior from these docs and neither implementation would look wrong.

## F4 — High. The trade-off is recorded nowhere

The job-queue Decisions table covers scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload size limit (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:29-33`) — the one choice that determines whether members lose work is the one choice with no row, and the HLD has no Key Design Decisions section at all to hold it. Under *decision capture* — a live decision needs to survive on the page, not just in the author's head — this is the classic case: a cold reader holding these docs would question the choice and probably reverse it, and cannot reconstruct what was given up (durability for simplicity) or what was accepted (members resubmit). There's a third problem layered on: `QUEUE-004` covers rebuilding farm state by polling printers at startup (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md:6`), which makes the loss of accepted-but-undispatched jobs look like an intended part of restart recovery rather than an unreconciled gap. Under *effective intent-tree alignment* — an LLD behavior with no spec drifts silently — the loss behavior itself has no spec, which is what lets the conflict sit unnoticed.

## Your three ways out

These are genuinely your call, and each has a different downstream cost:

**Make the design honor the tenet.** Persist accepted jobs (a SQLite file alongside the registry's, or an append-only queue log) and restore them on startup. Note that G-code files are already kept on disk under a content hash (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`), so the "keep things on disk" machinery partly exists. Cost: LLD Architecture rewrite, a new spec (something like "When the service restarts, the system SHALL restore accepted, undispatched jobs to the front of their class queue"), and a line in the HLD if you want the durability guarantee stated at architecture level. Note that rebuilding from printers still has to stay — only a durable store knows about jobs that were accepted but never dispatched; printers can only tell you what they're running now.

**Record the exception and keep the design.** A Decisions row that names the tenet being traded against ("a submitted job is a promise"), says plainly that accepted-but-undispatched jobs are lost on restart, and gives the why (mini-PC footprint, in-memory simplicity). This closes both F3 and F4 with the least churn, and it still needs a spec so the behavior is testable rather than prose. Per the skill's guidance this would be the minimum sufficient capture; a fuller decision doc is worth considering only if you want the option space (in-memory vs SQLite vs append-only log) on the record for the next reader.

**Revise the tenet to what the design can honor.** Something like "accepted jobs are best-effort; resubmission is cheap because files are content-hashed" — which is true today — and then push the member-facing promise somewhere it can actually be kept (the web UI, or a resubmit affordance). This is the honest route if the durability loss is genuinely a constraint you accept; the cost is that the HLD's goal "morning pickup — with no member present" needs revisiting too, because it presumes the overnight window survives.

One timing note in your favor: there's no implementation yet, so this is a doc-only change today. Once the queue and its restart path are coded, the same fix becomes a schema change plus a recovery path plus a migration story.
