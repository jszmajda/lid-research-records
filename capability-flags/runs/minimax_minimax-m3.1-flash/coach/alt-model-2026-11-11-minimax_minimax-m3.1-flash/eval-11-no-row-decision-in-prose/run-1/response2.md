**F2 — High. The job queue holds accepted jobs only in process memory, so a restart silently drops every accepted-but-undispatched job.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` states the design twice in one sentence: the queue is "held entirely in process memory," and "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The Context section names the trigger conditions — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" (`job-queue-design.md:10`) — so the loss path is anticipated by the design, not accidental. What is missing is the other half: the tenet says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`), and with a volatile queue a member who uploads and walks away loses the job before anyone else is awake. This is *tenet cascade* — the HLD's tenets say which way to lean when a decision has two defensible answers, and the mechanism here runs the wrong way from the tenet — combined with *decision capture* — a choice a cold reader would still question ("why did we accept data loss here?") survives only in prose, with no Decisions row naming the tenet traded against and why. Leaving it as-is costs you twice: the promise in the HLD quietly stops being true, and every future session that touches the queue has to re-derive whether losing jobs is intended before it can safely change anything near dispatch.

**What I'd recommend.** You have three honest options, and which one is right depends on a truth only you hold — whether "upload and walk away" is a literal promise to members or a statement of direction that exceptions are acceptable:

1. **Change the design** — persist the accepted queue (accepted-job metadata durably; the G-code already lives on disk under a content hash at `job-queue-design.md:14`, so the store is half-built), and keep the tenet literal.
2. **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row to `job-queue-design.md` naming the tenet traded against ("a submitted job is a promise"), the cost accepted ("accepted jobs are lost on restart; members resubmit"), and why (mini-PC simplicity, no durable queue store). Recording the exception also closes the decision-capture half of this finding, since the row becomes the place a cold reader looks.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if loss-on-restart is genuinely acceptable for this makerspace, soften the HLD tenet to match the shipped intent, so the HLD stops promising something the design doesn't deliver.

**Two practical notes.** First, this is a good moment to settle it: the repo has no code or tests yet (F1), so whichever way you go, nothing has to be unwritten — but by the time the queue is implemented, this choice gets expensive to reverse. Second, whichever option you pick, the EARS layer needs to say it out loud: today there is no spec for durable acceptance or for job loss on restart, so the behavior lives entirely in LLD prose. If you keep the in-memory design, a spec like "When the service restarts, the system SHALL discard accepted-but-undispatched jobs and require resubmission" would make the cost legible to any agent that later greps for it — and if you move to durable acceptance, `QUEUE-004` is the natural place to extend it, since it already covers rebuilding farm state on startup.
