**F2 (high) — the queue holds accepted jobs only in memory, and that quietly breaks your first tenet.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` states the queue is "a per-printer-class list of accepted jobs held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them"; the same doc's Context says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" (`:10`), so the loss condition is not hypothetical — it is named in your own design. Against that, `docs/high-level-design.md:20` promises "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and `:26` makes "overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" a goal. Under *tenet cascade* — do the design nodes lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean — this is the project working against itself: the member who uploaded at 9pm and went to bed is exactly the person the promise was written for, and a 2am power blip silently un-promises it. Two things compound if this sits: nothing in the EARS layer records the loss at all (no spec says jobs are dropped on restart), so an implementer reading only the specs could build either a durable queue or an in-memory one and both look conformant; and because the G-code files are already kept on disk under a content hash (`:14`), the durable design is now the *smaller* one, so the cost of the current choice keeps falling while the risk stays. Under *decision capture* — a choice a cold reader would still question should leave a row they can reconstruct the trade-off from — this one has no row at all: a future session reading the LLD finds a load-bearing architectural choice stated as a throwaway clause in prose, with no rejected alternative, no accepted cost, and no pointer to the tenet it trades against.

Your three options (this one is yours to pick, not mine):

- **Change the design** — persist the queue. The cheapest shape fits what your prose already assumes: keep only job metadata (slug, class, priority, content hash, enqueue time) in the same SQLite file the registry already uses, and let the already-persisted G-code on disk supply the payload. QUEUE-004 already handles rebuilding *running* state by polling printers; what would need extending is rebuilding *queued* state. This restores the promise outright and costs a little of the "service stays small" tenet, which is a trade the tenet ordering would then have to permit.
- **Record the exception** — keep memory-only, but capture it: a Decisions row in `job-queue-design.md` naming the tenet traded against ("a submitted job is a promise"), the rejected alternatives (SQLite-backed queue, journal-to-disk on enqueue), and the accepted cost (a restart loses accepted jobs; members resubmit; resubmission is cheap because the file is hash-addressed on disk). Then fix the specs to match reality — a spec that says the system SHALL drop queued jobs on restart, so the behavior is tested rather than folklore. This one remedy closes both the tenet-conflict and decision-capture halves of the finding.
- **Revise the tenet** — narrow it to something the design actually guarantees, e.g. "an uploaded job is a promise until the printer accepts it," and let the LLD and specs stay as they are. Honest, but it trades away the promise that makes the evening-upload workflow worth building, so I'd want you to name that cost explicitly.
