**The core issue — the behavior exists in prose but has no spec (F2).** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` states that the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the uploaded G-code itself *is* kept on disk under a content hash. So the durability boundary already exists in your design: the expensive bytes survive, the queue metadata (position, priority class, target class, owner) does not. No spec says any of this. `QUEUE-004` (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md:6`) covers rebuilding *printer* state on startup, and a cold reader who greps it will reasonably infer "the service recovers its state on boot" — the trap is that the accepted-job queue is the one thing that does not recover, and that gap is invisible unless you read the LLD. This sits under *effective intent-tree alignment — specs and LLDs agree, and LLD behaviors with no spec are unnamed intent that drifts silently*: every future session that touches the queue will either re-derive this rule from the prose or quietly assume the queue survives, and those two readings produce different code.

**Why it matters more than it looks — the restart is routine, not exceptional.** Your own LLD context (`job-queue-design.md:10`) says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." So "restart" is a normal event in this deployment, and each one silently discards every job that was accepted but not yet dispatched — which, given the evening-upload/overnight-print goal (`docs/high-level-design.md:26`), is precisely the window when the queue is full. Worse, the loss is invisible to the member: they uploaded and walked away, and the only signal is that nothing prints in the morning. This directly cuts against the HLD's first tenet — *a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away* (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`). And there's no Decisions row recording the tradeoff: `job-queue-design.md:27-33` covers scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload size, but the durability choice survives only as a clause in the architecture paragraph — no rejected alternative, no accepted cost, nothing a future reader could reconstruct.

**Three ways to settle it — the decision is yours, but the arrow needs one of them on the page.** (1) *Persist the queue.* Since the G-code is already on disk, this means persisting a small amount of metadata — job slug, class, priority, position, content hash — at accept time. The registry already uses SQLite (`printer-registry-design.md:14`), so a sibling `queue.db` fits the mini-PC and the "service stays small" tenet; the one wrinkle is that a durable queue needs idempotence on resubmission (a member whose job *did* survive and who resubmits anyway should not get two prints) — the content hash you already compute is the natural key. (2) *Keep the in-memory queue and make the loss explicit.* If losing overnight jobs is genuinely acceptable for a makerspace that costs little to re-slice, that's a legitimate choice — but it needs a spec stating it (`When the service restarts, the system SHALL discard accepted but undispatched jobs and SHALL ...`), a member-visible signal rather than silence, and a Decisions row naming the tenet being traded against and why. (3) *Revise the tenet* if the promise was never meant to survive a restart — say so explicitly in the HLD rather than leaving it implied.

**What I'd suggest, and where to put it.** If the makerspace's members upload in the evening and collect in the morning (your stated goal), option (1) is the one I'd put in front of you — the persistence cost is small because the bytes are already stored, and it converts a silent overnight loss into a non-event. Whichever you choose, the concrete steps are: settle the behavior with you first; write the spec into `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md` as a new `QUEUE-006` (IDs are stable — append, don't renumber); add the matching row to the Decisions & Alternatives table in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` naming the rejected alternative and the accepted cost; and, if the queue becomes durable, resolve F3 at the same time — if a job's dispatch state is persisted but printer state is still owned by both nodes, restart recovery gets ambiguous about which printer owns what.
