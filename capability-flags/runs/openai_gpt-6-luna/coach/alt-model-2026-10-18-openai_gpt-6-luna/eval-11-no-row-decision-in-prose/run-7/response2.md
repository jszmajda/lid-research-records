The queue LLD says accepted jobs that have not been dispatched are held in process memory and disappear on restart; startup recovery only polls printers for jobs already running. The G-code files persist under content hashes, so the file may still be on disk, but the queue loses the record that connects it to an accepted job. “Resubmitting is cheap” reduces the recovery effort, but still asks members to notice the loss and act—at odds with the HLD’s promise that they can submit a job and walk away.

This is a *tenet cascade* concern: the component design should uphold the HLD’s unattended-printing promise, or explicitly record why it does not. My recommendation is to preserve the promise by making accepted-but-not-yet-finished job records durable. Since the G-code files are already stored on disk and the design already uses SQLite for the registry, SQLite-backed queue metadata seems like a proportionate fit for the mini-PC. The queue LLD should specify what gets restored and how startup reconciles jobs whose dispatch may have reached a printer just before a restart, so recovery doesn’t accidentally lose or duplicate work.

The storage choice is also an under-captured decision: the LLD states the loss behavior but not why that cost was chosen, and its Decisions table has no row for queue durability. Under *docs carry current intent*—docs should explain choices a cold reader might still question—I’d add a concise row recording the chosen durability policy, the alternative, and the accepted cost. Then add EARS specs for the restart behavior and recovery of accepted jobs, with tests that exercise a restart and the uncertain-dispatch case.

If the intended product behavior is instead “members must resubmit after a restart,” that can be a valid tradeoff—but I’d make the promise and goal explicit about that limitation, and specify how members learn their jobs need resubmitting.
