The queue design currently keeps accepted-job records only in memory, while storing uploaded G-code files on disk by content hash (`job-queue-design.md`, “Architecture”). On restart, it polls printers to discover jobs already running, but any accepted job that had not yet been dispatched is lost and the member must resubmit it. So the file may still be on disk, but the queue no longer has the record that connects it to its class, priority, or place in line.

That conflicts with the HLD’s tenet that “a submitted job is a promise” and with the stated goal of unattended evening uploads. The risk is especially relevant because the design itself calls out power interruptions and unattended OS updates. Under *tenet cascade*—component designs should follow the HLD’s stated preferences—the queue needs either to preserve accepted work across restarts or to explicitly record why this promise has an exception.

**I recommend making accepted jobs durable.** Persist the queue record and ordering alongside the existing on-disk G-code, then restore queued work on startup and reconcile it with printers before dispatching. Also define how a restart during dispatch is handled so recovery doesn’t accidentally send a job twice. This fits the reliability promise while keeping the implementation local to the mini-PC. The exact storage mechanics can stay open until the design phase.

To make that intent testable, add EARS specs for surviving a restart and preserving queue order, then write tests for restart recovery and dispatch recovery before changing the implementation. Record the persistence choice and its tradeoffs in the queue’s Decisions & Alternatives table; currently, the memory-only choice is stated in the architecture but its rationale isn’t captured there. If you instead prefer memory-only queues, I’d recommend recording that as an explicit exception to the promise and clarifying what members should expect after a restart.
