The key detail is that **the uploaded G-code file is saved on disk, but the accepted job’s place in the queue is kept only in memory**. The queue design says that after a restart, the service polls printers to discover jobs already running, but accepted jobs that were waiting to dispatch are lost and members must resubmit (`job-queue-design.md`, “Architecture”; `QUEUE-004` covers the printer polling). So a file may still exist on disk without the service knowing that it was accepted, which class it belongs to, or where it stood in the queue.

That conflicts with the HLD’s promise that members can upload and walk away, including for overnight printing. A restart or power interruption could silently undo an acceptance and leave a member expecting a print that will never start. The design also doesn’t explain why that tradeoff is acceptable.

**I’d first settle the durability promise.** If “upload and walk away” means an accepted job must survive a restart, then persistence is the path most consistent with the HLD: save the accepted job record and its queue position before acknowledging acceptance, then restore pending jobs at startup and reconcile jobs already running on printers. The specs and tests should pin down that behavior, including preserving order and avoiding duplicate dispatch after recovery.

If you instead want to keep the queue in memory, make the tradeoff explicit: record why the loss risk is acceptable and revise the promise or goal so “accepted” doesn’t imply restart durability. Those are design choices for you; I wouldn’t leave the current contradiction implicit.
