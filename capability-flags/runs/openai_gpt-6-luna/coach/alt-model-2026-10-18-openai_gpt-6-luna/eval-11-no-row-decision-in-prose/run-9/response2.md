The queue design distinguishes between jobs **already running** and jobs **accepted but not yet dispatched**. On startup, `job-queue-design.md` says the service polls printers to recover running jobs, but accepted jobs still waiting in the in-memory queue are lost; members must resubmit them. The uploaded G-code files remain on disk, but the queue’s accepted-job state does not. This matters because the same design calls out unattended OS updates and power blips, while the HLD says a submitted job should be a promise and sets overnight printing as a goal.

That’s a *tenet cascade* concern—lower-level design should follow the HLD’s stated preferences. I’d recommend resolving this at the design level before treating the queue behavior as complete. There are a few coherent choices: persist accepted jobs so they can be restored after restart; explicitly define acceptance as non-durable and make the promise/UX reflect that; or revise the tenet if it means something narrower. Durable queue state appears to fit the unattended-printing goal, but the intended tradeoff is yours to choose.

There’s also a related *decision capture* gap—important choices should carry their rationale in the design, not just describe behavior in prose. The restart-loss behavior has no Decisions & Alternatives row explaining why that cost is acceptable against the promise tenet. Once you choose a direction, update `job-queue-design.md` to capture the choice and rationale, then align `job-queue-specs.md`—especially `QUEUE-004`—with the resulting recovery behavior. For durable acceptance, make the acceptance point and what startup restores explicit; for intentional loss, specify that behavior and its user-facing consequences.
