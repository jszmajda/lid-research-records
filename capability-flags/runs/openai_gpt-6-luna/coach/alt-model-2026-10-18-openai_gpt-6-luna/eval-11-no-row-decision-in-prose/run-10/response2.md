The queue design makes the risk concrete: `job-queue-design.md` says the queue exists only in memory, and that accepted jobs not yet dispatched are lost on restart. The same section notes unattended OS updates and power interruptions. Meanwhile, the HLD calls a submitted job “a promise” and describes members uploading and walking away. So after a restart, a member may believe their job is queued when it has vanished. `QUEUE-004` covers polling printers for jobs already running, but not recovering accepted jobs that were still waiting.

That’s a *tenet cascade* concern: the detailed design should uphold the HLD’s promise under the operating conditions it describes. It’s also a decision-capture gap: the LLD describes the in-memory choice, but doesn’t explain why its cost—requiring members to resubmit after a restart—is acceptable.

**My recommendation, given the current HLD, is to make accepted pending jobs durable.** A local store such as SQLite may fit the mini-PC and the project’s existing SQLite choice, but the design should decide whether that’s the same database or a separate one. The LLD and specs would then define when a job counts as accepted, how its queue position and priority survive restart, and how startup reconciliation avoids duplicating work already running on a printer. Tests should cover a restart after acceptance but before dispatch, as well as recovery around dispatch.

If requiring resubmission is intentional, the other coherent path is to make that limitation explicit and revisit the HLD promise and unattended-print goal. Either way, capture the tradeoff in the LLD’s Decisions & Alternatives table: what was chosen, what alternative was considered, and what cost the project accepts. Which matters more for printfarm: preserving the “upload and walk away” promise, or keeping the queue volatile and asking members to resubmit after interruptions?
