The key issue is that `job-queue-design.md` says the accepted-job queue lives entirely in process memory, and that accepted jobs not yet dispatched are lost on restart. The G-code files are kept on disk, but the design doesn’t say the pending queue can be reconstructed from them. So an OS update or power blip could erase a member’s overnight job after the service accepted it. That conflicts with the HLD’s “submitted job is a promise” tenet and the goal of morning pickup without the member present.

I’d recommend making the meaning of that promise explicit: **should an accepted, not-yet-dispatched job survive a service restart?** Given the HLD as written, survival seems like the more coherent fit. If that is the intended guarantee, update the queue design to specify durable pending-job state and restart recovery, then add EARS coverage and tests for that behavior before implementation. The recovery design should account for the fact that startup already polls printers for jobs they are currently running.

If losing queued jobs on restart is an intentional simplicity trade-off for the mini-PC, that can be a valid choice too—but the docs should make the exception clear. Record the tenet being traded against and the accepted cost in the queue design’s Decisions & Alternatives table, and consider adjusting the promise or member-facing guarantee so it doesn’t imply restart safety. That would also address the decision-capture finding.
