**F1 — The queue’s restart behavior conflicts with the HLD’s promise.** In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md`, the queue exists only in memory, so a restart drops jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched; the G-code files remain on disk, but the pending queue records do not. That matters especially because the HLD mentions occasional power blips and says a submitted job is a promise members should be able to walk away from. This is a *tenet cascade* issue: a component’s behavior is working against a project-level preference under a condition the design already anticipates. I’d first settle what “accepted” promises. If accepted jobs must survive restarts, update the queue design and specify recovery behavior; if losing them is an intentional tradeoff, record the exception and make the promise’s limits clear. The existing `QUEUE-004` covers polling printers for currently running jobs, but doesn’t settle what happens to pending jobs.

**F2 — The persistence tradeoff is described but not captured as a decision.** The design says pending jobs are lost and members resubmit, but its Decisions & Alternatives table doesn’t explain why that was chosen or what cost is accepted. Under *docs carry current intent*—a cold reader should be able to understand why the design is the way it is—I’d add a decision row if you keep this behavior, naming the alternative and the reason for accepting the loss. If you instead choose restart recovery, update the design and add a spec for recovering pending jobs; then add a test that restarts with a queued, undispatched job and verifies the intended outcome.

My recommendation is to resolve the meaning of **accepted** before changing implementation: should an accepted job still be queued after a service restart, including a power blip? If yes, durable queue recovery fits the HLD promise. If no, document that exception and clarify what members are promised.
