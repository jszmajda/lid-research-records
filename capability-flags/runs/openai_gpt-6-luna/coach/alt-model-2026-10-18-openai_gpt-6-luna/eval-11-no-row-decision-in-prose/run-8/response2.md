In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md`, accepted jobs live only in process memory. On startup, the service rebuilds state by asking printers what they’re currently running; jobs accepted but not yet dispatched are lost, even though their uploaded G-code files may still be on disk under a content hash. Members must resubmit those jobs after a restart.

That creates a real tension with the HLD’s promise that members can upload and walk away—especially since the design calls out unattended updates and power interruptions. Under *tenet cascade*—component designs should honor the HLD’s preferences, or explicitly record an exception—this behavior needs a deliberate decision. The related *decision capture* issue is that the LLD describes the loss, but doesn’t explain why it’s an acceptable tradeoff against that promise.

Given the current HLD, I’d recommend exploring a **durable queue** first. The project already uses SQLite for the registry, and persisting accepted-job metadata there may fit the “small service” tenet. The design should also account for recovery around dispatch: after a restart, reconcile persisted queue state with printer-reported state so a job isn’t silently lost or dispatched twice.

A different choice could be valid, but it should be explicit: retain the loss and record why the promise has an exception, or revise the promise if it no longer reflects the intended behavior. I’d resolve that in the queue LLD, add EARS specs for acceptance and restart recovery, then define tests for a restart before dispatch and during dispatch before changing the code.
