**F2 — High. The queue can lose accepted work on restart.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` says the queue exists entirely in memory and that accepted but undispatched jobs are lost after a restart; the same design describes unattended OS updates and power interruptions, while the HLD promises members can upload and walk away. Under *tenet cascade*—component behavior should uphold the project’s promises in the conditions its design describes—that leaves a gap between what “accepted” appears to promise and what the system guarantees. The G-code file remains on disk, but the queue entry and its place in line do not, so members may have to resubmit and the service may also leave uploaded files with no queued job.

My recommendation is to decide explicitly what acceptance guarantees, then align the queue design and specs with that choice. For the stated upload-and-walk-away goal, the strongest fit is to persist accepted job metadata and ordering in queue-owned storage, while keeping the G-code payload in its existing content-addressed files. On startup, restore pending jobs and reconcile dispatched or running jobs with the printers; specify how recovery avoids losing or dispatching a job twice. Add specs for those guarantees, then tests citing them before implementation. The registry’s SQLite choice is in a sibling design node, so whether the queue shares that database should be treated as a separate cross-component decision.

If the intended behavior is instead that members must resubmit after a restart, make that limitation explicit in the promise and record the tradeoff in the queue’s Decisions & Alternatives table. Under *docs carry current intent*—design docs should state today’s intent and preserve the reasoning a future reader needs—that record will keep future work from silently assuming accepted jobs survive interruptions.
