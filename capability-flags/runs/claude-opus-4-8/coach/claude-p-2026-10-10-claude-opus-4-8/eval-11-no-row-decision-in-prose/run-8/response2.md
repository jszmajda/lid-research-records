These two findings are really one problem seen from two angles — the architecture choice itself, and the fact that it was never written down as a choice. Let me take each.

**F2 — High. The in-memory queue silently drops a member's accepted job under a restart the design itself treats as routine.** The job-queue LLD says the queue is held "entirely in process memory," and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md`, Architecture). The same section's Context names exactly when that happens: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." So the triggering condition isn't an exotic failure — it's a normal overnight event, during precisely the window the service exists to cover. This runs against two things the project declared about itself: the tenet *a submitted job is a promise — members should be able to upload and walk away*, and the goal *evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup, with no member present*. This is the *tenet cascade* lens — a leaf mechanism should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean, and here, under a condition these docs describe, it does the opposite. Why it matters: the member who most relies on the promise (uploaded at night, went home) is the one the design quietly fails, and because it "resubmit them" is buried in prose, nobody reasoning about the queue later will notice the promise has a hole in it until a member complains about a part that never printed. The resolution is genuinely yours to make — I'd lay out three options and not pick for you: (a) **change the design** so accepted-but-undispatched jobs survive a restart (e.g., persist the pending queue the same way the registry already persists to SQLite, or journal accepted jobs to the disk store you already keep G-code in under a content hash); (b) **record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet/goal being traded against and why volatility is acceptable (e.g., restarts are rare enough, resubmission is cheap because files are content-hashed); or (c) **revise the tenet** if "walk away" was always meant to tolerate a resubmit. Any of the three closes the drift; they just close it in different directions.

**F3 — High. That volatility trade-off exists only as prose, never as a captured decision.** Every other load-bearing call in that LLD earns a Decisions & Alternatives row that names the rejected alternative and the accepted cost — FIFO ("shortest-job-first rejected… accepted cost: one long job can hold a class"), base32 slugs ("UUIDs rejected… slugs recycle after 90 days"), the 64 MB limit. The in-memory-queue decision — arguably the most consequential of the four, since it's the one that can break the headline promise — has no row at all. Under *docs carry current intent, written to be read cold* (the keep-side: a choice a cold reader would still question belongs in the Decisions table with its alternative and cost), this is an under-captured decision. Why it matters: a future session reading the Architecture prose sees "jobs are gone, members resubmit" stated as a flat fact, with no signal that durability was *considered and declined* versus simply never thought about. That ambiguity is exactly what makes the choice hard to revisit safely — the next person can't tell whether they'd be correcting an oversight or overturning a deliberate call. The remedy is small: a single Decisions & Alternatives row in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` — *Decision:* queue durability across restarts; *Chosen:* in-memory, jobs re-submitted after restart; *Rationale:* persistent-queue rejected because [your reason]; accepted cost: undispatched jobs lost on restart, mitigated by content-hashed resubmission. A full decision doc would be overkill here — this is one row, not a debate on the record.

Note the overlap: if you take option (b) for F2 — recording the exception as a Decisions row — that *same row* satisfies F3. You'd close both with one edit. If you instead take option (a) and make the queue durable, F3 still wants a row documenting that you chose durability and why, but the tenet conflict dissolves on its own.
