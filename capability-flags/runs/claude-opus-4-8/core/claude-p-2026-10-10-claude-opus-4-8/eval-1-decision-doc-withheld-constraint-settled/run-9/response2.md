Overkill, in my read — the row is the right home.

The test isn't how close the options felt while you were deciding (the skill is explicit that a choice that was contested but lands obvious needs no doc). It's whether a cold reader of the *result* would re-litigate it. Here they wouldn't: two of the HLD tenets — "no runtime network access is permitted" and "kiosks have no network route during normal operation" — exclude both the HTTPS directory and the managed service outright. Once the choice lands next to those tenets, an on-kiosk backend reads as forced, not as one live option among three. A cold reader asking "why SQLite?" is answered by a single line, which is exactly what a Decisions row is for.

A full decision doc earns its place when the alternatives stay genuinely credible against competing criteria after landing — so a reader *would* reverse it without the options and trade-offs written out. That's not this: the criteria that made them feel close (index-maintenance burden, richer search later) all lose to a hard constraint, so they don't stay live.

One thing worth noting: the reason the alternatives collapse is the offline tenets. If there were any chance those tenets loosen later — some future kiosk gets a lab-network route — then the trade-off reopens and a doc would start to earn its keep.
