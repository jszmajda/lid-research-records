Honest answer: I don't think it earns one — and the 180-message thread is exactly the reason the rule tells me to set that feeling aside.

The test for a decision doc is applied from the **landed state, not the deliberation**: would a cold reader of the finished design question 2Q or be tempted to reverse it *without* the competing options in front of them? Not "was it hard to agree on?" A choice that was brutal to settle but reads as sensible once it lands needs no doc — writing one up is just memorializing the argument, which is the residue LID strips.

Two things keep this on the row side of the line:

1. **It affects only its own node.** The external interface, render-queue concurrency, and disk budget are all explicitly unchanged — nothing else gets sized around 2Q. The rule says don't even offer a doc for a choice that's contained to one node, however long the debate ran.
2. **The row already carries what a cold reader needs.** The three-way comparison — 2Q vs LRU vs aged-frequency, and the "accept bookkeeping to protect comparison sheets during screenings" tradeoff — fits in the Decisions & Alternatives row I added. A cold reader can reconstruct the "why" and won't be tempted to flip it. A full doc would just be that same row, longer.

So my recommendation is to leave it as the row. A decision doc here would be a standing invitation to re-open the debate every time someone reads it, which is the opposite of what you want after three meetings.
