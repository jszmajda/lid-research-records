**F1 — High. The volatile in-memory queue trades against the "submitted job is a promise" tenet under conditions the docs themselves name.**

`job-queue-design.md` describes the queue as "a per-printer-class list of accepted jobs held entirely in process memory," then a sentence later notes that the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." The consequence: a member uploads in the evening, walks away trusting the tenet, and comes back in the morning to find the job never ran — not because printing failed, but because the service restarted and silently dropped the queue. The HLD's first tenet ("a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away") says exactly what this scenario violates, and nothing in the design records that the tenet was weighed and traded. Under *tenet cascade* — does a mechanism work against a tenet under a described operating condition? — this is the pattern to catch before implementation crystallizes it, because the cost of discovering the tension after the queue is built is a re-architecture decision rather than a design-time choice. There are three ways to resolve it, and the choice is yours: change the design (persist accepted jobs to disk before acknowledging the upload), record the exception (a Decisions row naming "submitted job is a promise" as the tenet traded against, and why the restart-loss tradeoff is acceptable — perhaps because jobs are cheap to resubmit and the mini-PC complexity savings are worth it), or revise the tenet (scope "promise" to mean "once dispatched" rather than "once accepted"). Recording the exception would also close F3.

---

**F3 — Medium. The volatile-queue choice has no Decisions row, so a cold reader encounters it with no rationale offered.**

The architectural decision that the queue lives entirely in process memory — and the accepted consequence that a restart discards accepted-but-undispatched jobs — is one of the most consequential design choices in the service. It sets expectations for members, shapes failure modes, and determines whether the mini-PC's unattended-restart behavior is a bug or an accepted cost. The `job-queue-design.md` Decisions table already has good rows (scheduling order, job identifiers, upload size limit), but this choice is absent. A future session reading these docs cold encounters the volatile-queue fact in the Architecture section but finds no row explaining why it was chosen over persisting to disk, what was given up, and what justified the trade. Under *decision capture* — a choice a cold reader would still question, with only prose and no row — this is exactly the pattern that silently erodes intent over sessions: the reasoning exists somewhere in the conversation that produced the design, but it never landed on the page. The fix is a fuller Decisions row, something like: "Queue persistence | In-process memory only | Persisting to SQLite rejected — adds startup complexity for an edge case members can mitigate by resubmitting; accepted cost: accepted-but-undispatched jobs are lost on restart." If the resolution of F1 involves recording the tenet exception, that same row covers this finding too.
