**F1 — High. The in-memory queue design conflicts with the "submitted job is a promise" tenet under conditions the LLD itself describes as normal.**

The job-queue LLD's context section says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Later the architecture section says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." Set those two facts next to the HLD's first tenet — "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" — and a conflict surfaces: a member who uploads on a Tuesday evening and goes home has no guarantee that overnight OS maintenance won't silently drop their job. The goal makes it concrete: "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup." Under a routine restart, that goal fails without notice. Under *tenet cascade* — leaf designs should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean — this is exactly the pattern the principle exists to catch: a mechanism that works against a stated commitment under a condition the project's own docs describe. The remedy here is yours to choose: you could add queue persistence (SQLite is already on the table for the registry), change the tenet to honestly reflect that jobs are best-effort across restarts, or record a deliberate exception in a Decisions row naming the tenet you're trading against and why the simplicity is worth it. Any of those closes the conflict; leaving the current text means every future agent reading the HLD and the LLD together will either miss the tension or re-derive it and be uncertain whether it was intentional.

**F2 — High. The persistence choice has no Decisions row, leaving a consequential gap on the record.**

The job-queue LLD does have a Decisions table, and it's put to good use — the FIFO-vs-SJF call is well captured with the alternative named and the accepted cost acknowledged. But the choice to hold the queue in memory rather than persist it is not there, even though it is arguably the most consequential decision in that LLD. A cold reader would immediately ask: the registry already uses SQLite; why doesn't the queue? What was given up, and what was the cost accepted? Right now there is no answer on the page. Under *decision capture* — choices a cold reader would question or be tempted to reverse should have their reasoning recorded — this is a gap worth closing. The smallest fix is a new row in the Decisions table naming the rejected alternative (e.g., SQLite persistence) and the accepted cost (members resubmit after restart). If you resolve F1 by adding persistence, this row goes away on its own. If you resolve F1 by recording an exception or revising the tenet, the row is the record. Either way, the two findings share a remedy path — working through F1 will naturally close F2.
