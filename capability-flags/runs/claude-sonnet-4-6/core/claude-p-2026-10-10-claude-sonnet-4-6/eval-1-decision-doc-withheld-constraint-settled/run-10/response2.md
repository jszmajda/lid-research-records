Overkill. The test for a decision doc is whether a cold reader of the *result* would find the choice non-obvious and be tempted to reverse it. A cold reader who reaches the Decisions row will see the tenet right above it in the HLD: "All serial-number lookup SHALL complete using files on the kiosk; no runtime network access is permitted." That tenet eliminates both alternatives outright — it's not a close judgment call that requires criteria and trade-off narrative to reconstruct, it's an exclusion. The row in the LLD captures the rationale in one line, which is all that's needed.

The deliberation felt close while you were in it because the three options look symmetric at first glance. But the tenet makes the answer structural once you see it. That's exactly the situation where a decision doc adds no value: the choice doesn't stay live for a future reader.
