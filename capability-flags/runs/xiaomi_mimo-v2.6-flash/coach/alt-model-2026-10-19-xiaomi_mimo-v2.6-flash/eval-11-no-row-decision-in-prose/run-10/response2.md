**F2 — High. The in-memory queue defeats the promise tenet under conditions the docs themselves describe.** job-queue-design.md:14 states the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the Context two paragraphs up names "the occasional power blip" as a normal operating condition, and the HLD's first tenet says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away." Under *tenet cascade* — do the design nodes lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean? — these two lines collide the moment you hold the tenet next to the mechanism: the member's defining workflow is an evening upload with nobody watching, so a 3 a.m. reboot silently discards queued jobs and the member isn't present to discover or resubmit them until morning. The conflict sits unrecorded: no Decisions row, no decision doc, nothing in the registry or HLD noting that durability was traded away. Leaving it this way means every future session that touches dispatch, restart handling, or the upload flow meets the contradiction cold and has to guess which side is authoritative — the tenet or the Architecture paragraph — and plausible guesses will run in both directions. There's also a live design question underneath: printer-registry already persists to a single SQLite file, so the durability path exists one node over, which makes "in-memory because the mini-PC is modest" read less like a forced constraint and more like a choice someone could reasonably reverse.
