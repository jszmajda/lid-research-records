**F2 — High. The queue's durability design argues with your own promise tenet.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` states the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the same node's `Context` (line 10) names the operating condition that makes this live: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." That's *tenet cascade* (design mechanisms should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean): `docs/high-level-design.md:20` says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the first goal is "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." A member who uploads at 9pm and a power blip at 2am means the promise fails exactly in the scenario the HLD exists for, and the design's answer — "members resubmit them" — quietly moves the work back onto a member who, per the tenet, should never have to check. No Decisions row or decision doc records this exception, so a future session reading the HLD and the LLD together sees a contradiction, not a trade-off, and will likely "fix" one side without knowing the other was deliberate. It also compounds asymmetrically: every later session must re-derive whether job loss is intended. One related edge — QUEUE-004 covers the restart *rebuild*, but no spec names the loss itself, so even if you accept the behavior, nothing pins it down as intent.
