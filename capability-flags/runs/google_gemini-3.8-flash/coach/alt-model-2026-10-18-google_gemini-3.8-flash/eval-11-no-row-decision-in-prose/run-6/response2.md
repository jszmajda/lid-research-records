**F1 — High. The in-memory queue mechanism loses undispatched jobs on restart, directly conflicting with the HLD's core goal and tenet under described operating conditions.** In `docs/high-level-design.md`, the primary goal is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" and the first tenet states "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away." In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md`, § Context notes that the workshop mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip," yet § Architecture specifies that the queue is held entirely in process memory such that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." Under *tenet cascade* — leaf design mechanisms must lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean — this creates an unmediated contradiction: an unattended overnight reboot drops accepted jobs while members are asleep, defeating the promise of unattended pickup. To resolve this, you have three options to choose from: (1) change the design so queued jobs survive restarts (such as persisting queue order in the SQLite store already used by `printer-registry` or journaling queue state on disk beside the G-code content hashes); (2) record an explicit exception in the Decisions & Alternatives table of `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` naming the tenet traded against, the accepted cost of lost jobs, and any mitigation (such as member notification); or (3) revise the HLD tenet and goal if overnight unattended durability is not actually intended.

**F2 — High. The architectural decision to keep accepted queue state in volatile process memory is unrecorded in Decisions & Alternatives.** While `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` documents decisions for scheduling order, slug identifiers, and upload limits, the choice to keep the queue in-memory despite reboot risks is mentioned only as body prose in § Architecture without an entry in § Decisions & Alternatives. Under *decision capture* — decisions that remain live to a cold reader must capture what was chosen, what was rejected, and what cost was accepted — this omission leaves future sessions without the context needed to understand why persistence was bypassed, especially since SQLite is already adopted in `printer-registry`. Leaving the rationale uncaptured makes the decision fragile: subsequent developers or agents are likely to either question the omission or re-implement persistence without knowing if in-memory queueing was a deliberate trade-off for simplicity on modest hardware. The recommended action is to add a row to the Decisions & Alternatives table in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` detailing the choice, rejected alternatives (e.g., SQLite table, append-only log), and accepted costs. If the team prefers to weigh formal persistence options and failure recovery on the record, authoring a dedicated decision doc under `docs/intent/job-queue/decisions/` is also an option. Note that recording this exception also satisfies the second remedy for F1.
