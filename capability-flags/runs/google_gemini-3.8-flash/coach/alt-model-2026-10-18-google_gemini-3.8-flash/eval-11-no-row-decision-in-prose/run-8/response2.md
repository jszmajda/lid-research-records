**F1 — High. In-memory queue discard behavior conflicts with the HLD "upload and walk away" tenet and overnight print goal.** In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (Architecture), the queue is specified as held entirely in process memory, with accepted but undispatched jobs discarded on restart, while the Context section notes that the workshop mini-PC takes "unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — design nodes must lean the way upstream tenets and goals dictate — an overnight reboot under these operating conditions silently drops queued jobs, directly defeating the primary HLD goal (*"Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present"*) and contradicting the core tenet (*"A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away"*). When a downstream mechanism works against an upstream tenet without an explicit trade-off on record, agents and maintainers in future sessions encounter conflicting signals about reliability guarantees, and the system fails the user expectations the project was founded to satisfy. For remedies, LID leaves the choice to you among three paths:
1. **Change the design:** Persist the queue (for example, in SQLite like `printer-registry`, or as an on-disk manifest alongside the hashed G-code files) so accepted jobs survive restarts.
2. **Record the exception:** Keep the in-memory queue, but add an entry to the Decisions & Alternatives table in `job-queue-design.md` explicitly naming the tenet traded against (e.g., favoring the *"service stays small"* tenet or architectural simplicity) and accepting the cost of dropped jobs during overnight updates.
3. **Revise the upstream tenet or goal:** Update `docs/high-level-design.md` to clarify that job queuing is best-effort or requires active session supervision rather than an unattended promise.
