### The Findings in Detail

**F2 — High. Memory-only queue drops pre-dispatch jobs across restarts, conflicting with HLD tenets and goals.** In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (§Architecture, lines 14–15), the queue is specified as held entirely in process memory: on restart, "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." Yet the context notes that the workshop mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip," `docs/high-level-design.md` establishes Tenet 1 ("A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away"), and Goal 1 commits to "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." Under *tenet cascade* — design mechanisms must lean the way upstream tenets and goals direct — an in-memory queue silently breaks that promise whenever an unattended overnight reboot clears accepted jobs while the member is asleep. If left in place, downstream tests and implementation will cement a design that directly undermines the core value proposition of the system, forcing an expensive architectural rewrite later when members inevitably discover dropped prints.

**F4 — Medium. The in-memory storage choice lacks an explicit entry in Decisions & Alternatives.** While `docs/intent/printer-registry/printer-registry-design.md` explicitly records "Single SQLite file" in its Decisions & Alternatives table, `job-queue-design.md` records scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload limits, but omits the choice of in-memory queue state over persistence. Under *decision capture* — cold readers need explicit trade-offs and accepted costs for non-obvious architecture choices — a cold reader cannot reconstruct why the registry warrants SQLite while the queue (which holds the user's primary work units) drops state on restart, or what alternatives were considered. Without an explicit record, future sessions and contributors are likely to assume this was an oversight and attempt to re-architect it without understanding the original rationale.

---

### Recommendations

Because resolving a tenet conflict involves trade-offs, LID offers three standard paths rather than picking one for you:

1. **Align the design with the tenets (Recommended):** Persist accepted jobs to disk alongside the G-code uploads. Because the printer registry already uses SQLite on the mini-PC, `job-queue` could share or mirror that approach (or maintain a lightweight job manifest on disk). This ensures an unattended reboot or power blip preserves queued jobs and delivers on the "upload and walk away" promise. If you choose this, update `job-queue-design.md`, adjust or add EARS specs (e.g. recovering pending jobs on startup in `QUEUE-004`), and document the storage choice in Decisions & Alternatives.
2. **Record the exception:** If keeping the queue memory-only is a deliberate choice (for instance, to keep the service minimal and avoid state migration complexity on modest hardware), keep the design but record the exception explicitly in `job-queue-design.md` under Decisions & Alternatives. Name Tenet 1 and Goal 1 as the trade-offs accepted, and document how the system mitigates it (e.g. notifying members on restart). Recording this exception also resolves F4.
3. **Revise the HLD tenets and goals:** If the farm cannot realistically guarantee unattended persistence across restarts, revise Tenet 1 and Goal 1 in `docs/high-level-design.md` to reflect a best-effort queue where members must verify job status before pickup.
