**F1 — High. The queue's restart behaviour works against your first tenet.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` line 14 says the queue is "held entirely in process memory", and that after a restart, jobs accepted but not yet dispatched "are gone, and members resubmit them". Line 10 of the same doc says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip". Put together: a member uploads at 9pm, the box reboots for an update at 2am, and the job no longer exists. That defeats the HLD tenet "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" and the goal "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present". This is a *tenet cascade* finding — leaf designs should lean the way the HLD's tenets lean, or record the exception. It matters because tenets are what a future session uses to settle questions no spec covers. As written, an agent building job-queue has two authoritative documents pointing opposite ways and will pick one silently. There is a second, quieter cost: nothing says how a member learns their job was dropped, which also sits uneasily with "the queue explains itself". The content-hash store makes resubmitting cheap, but only for someone who knows they need to.

There are three ways to resolve this, and the choice is yours:

- **Change the design.** Persist accepted jobs so they survive a restart. The registry already keeps a SQLite file on the same box, so the cost against "the service stays small" may be low. `QUEUE-004` would then need to cover restoring the queue, not only polling printers.
- **Record the exception.** Keep the in-memory queue and add a Decisions row to `job-queue-design.md` that names the tenet traded against and why. If you go this way, the design probably also owes members some way to find out a job was lost.
- **Revise the tenet.** If the real promise is narrower (say, "a dispatched job is a promise"), change the tenet and the overnight goal in `docs/high-level-design.md` to say so.

**F2 — High. The in-memory choice has no Decisions row.** The job-queue Decisions table has rows for scheduling order, job identifiers and upload size, each naming what was rejected and what cost was accepted. The in-memory queue appears only in the Architecture prose, with no alternative and no rationale. This is a *decision capture* finding — a choice a cold reader would still question needs its trade-off on the page. A reader will question this one at once, because `printer-registry-design.md` persists to SQLite on the same machine and the tenet argues for durability. Without a row, the likely outcome is that a future session "fixes" it without knowing whether the choice was deliberate. The smallest capture that settles it is one fuller row: what was chosen, what was rejected (persisting to the registry's SQLite file is the obvious candidate), and what cost was accepted. A decision doc would be more than this needs. Note that if you take the "record the exception" route on F1, that same row closes F2. If you change the design instead, the row records the new choice.

**Two smaller findings ride along with whichever way you go.**

- **F7 — tenet order.** "A submitted job is a promise" and "the mini-PC is modest hardware; the service stays small" are exactly the two tenets in tension here, and the list is unordered. Ordering them in the HLD would let the next such question settle itself.
- **F4 — the missing spec.** The LLD describes job loss and resubmission on restart, but no spec states it. `QUEUE-004` covers only rebuilding farm state from the printers. Whatever you decide, the restart behaviour for queued jobs wants its own `QUEUE` spec so there is something to test against.

**Suggested sequence.** Decide F1 first, since it determines what the row, the spec and possibly the tenet say. Then make the change through `/linked-intent-dev` so it cascades from the HLD or LLD down to the specs in one pass. I haven't edited anything; the coach only advises.
