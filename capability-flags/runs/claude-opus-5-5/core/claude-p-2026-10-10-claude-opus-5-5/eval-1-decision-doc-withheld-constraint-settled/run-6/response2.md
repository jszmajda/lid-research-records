Overkill, I think. A decision doc earns its place when a future reader would be tempted to reopen the choice and needs the options and criteria to re-run the judgment. Here they can't reopen it: the HLD tenets say lookup must complete from files on the kiosk with no runtime network access, and that alone eliminates the HTTPS directory and the managed search service. One sentence settles "why SQLite?", and that sentence is already in the registry's Decisions table, with the two rejected options named.

The choice also stays inside the registry. Certificates sees the same `findBySerial` either way, so no other part of the design is sized around it.

The options felt close last session because the sketches compared them on maintenance, cost, and update size, and on those terms they are close. The no-network tenet is what breaks the tie, and it does so completely.

A doc would become worth writing if that tenet were ever up for revision, say if the kiosks gained a lab network route. Then the three options would be live again and the trade-offs would deserve a full write-up, at the HLD level rather than in the registry.
