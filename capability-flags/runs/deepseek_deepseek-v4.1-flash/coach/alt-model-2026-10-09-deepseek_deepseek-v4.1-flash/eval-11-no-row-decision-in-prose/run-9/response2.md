**F1 — High. The in-memory queue trades away the promise the HLD makes.**

The HLD's Tenets section opens with *"A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away,"* and its Goals section names the payoff — *"Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present."* The `job-queue` design then states the queue is *"held entirely in process memory,"* that *"jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them,"* and — in the same Context section — that the mini-PC *"takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip."* Put together, a power blip or an unattended reboot can silently swallow a member's submitted job, and the member only discovers it by coming in to nothing. Under *tenet cascade* — designs should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean, and a conflict with no recorded exception is drift — this is the pattern the dimension is built to catch: the tenet ("upload and walk away") and the mechanism ("jobs are gone on restart") never share a word, yet the mechanism defeats the tenet under exactly the operating condition the docs describe. It matters because the promise is the whole point of the service — "upload from home in the evening and collect the part the next day" is the problem statement the project exists to solve, and a design that can drop the job on a routine reboot turns the headline feature into a coin flip. Recording the exception — or changing the design, or revising the tenet — makes the trade visible so the next session doesn't silently re-decide it. I'd offer you three ways to close this rather than pick one for you: **change the design** so accepted jobs survive a restart (e.g., persist the accepted queue to the SQLite file the registry already keeps), **record the exception** in the LLD's Decisions & Alternatives naming the promise tenet traded against and why small footprint won, or **revise the tenet** to say jobs are best-effort across restarts. Which of those fits depends on whether "walk away" is actually load-bearing for your members.
