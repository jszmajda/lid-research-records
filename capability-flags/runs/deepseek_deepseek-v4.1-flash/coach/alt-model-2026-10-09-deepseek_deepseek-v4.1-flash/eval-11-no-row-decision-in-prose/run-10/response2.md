**F1 — High. The in-memory queue trades away the HLD's strongest tenet, and nothing records the trade.**

In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`, the design states plainly that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The HLD's tenet at `docs/high-level-design.md:20` says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and its goal is "morning pickup — with no member present." The two never share wording, which is exactly why this is easy to miss, but the design's own Context (`job-queue-design.md:10`) names "the occasional power blip" as an ordinary operating condition — and on that condition, an accepted job vanishes, the member has to resubmit, and a member is present again. That is the tenet and the goal both being spent, silently. Under *tenet cascade* — the design nodes should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean — the value here is that a future session reading the queue design cold would otherwise never learn a promise was softened to keep the service small; it would treat restart-loss as intended behavior and build on top of it. The remedy is yours to choose, and the skill deliberately doesn't pick one: you can **change the design** (persist accepted jobs so a restart doesn't drop them), **record the exception** (a Decisions & Alternatives row in `job-queue-design.md` naming "a submitted job is a promise" as the tenet traded against, and why the mini-PC hardware won), or **revise the tenet** if on reflection the promise is aspirational rather than guaranteed. If you record the exception, F2 below closes alongside it.
