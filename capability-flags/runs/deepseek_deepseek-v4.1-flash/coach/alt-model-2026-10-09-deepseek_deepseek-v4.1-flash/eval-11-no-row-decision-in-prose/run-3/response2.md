**F2 — High. The in-memory queue works against the "job is a promise" tenet.**

`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` states that the queue "held entirely in process memory," so "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The Context section two lines above (`:10`) names exactly the condition under which this bites — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." The HLD's first tenet is "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`), and its first goal is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (`:26`). A member who uploads at 9pm, loses their job to an overnight power blip, and discovers it in the morning has had the promise broken without any signal — which is the failure mode the tenet exists to prevent. Under *tenet cascade* — hold the HLD's tenets in mind and ask whether a leaf mechanism works against one under an operating condition the docs describe — this is a genuine conflict, and it's the kind that's hardest to catch because the tenet and the mechanism share no wording. The design's own Decisions table (`:29–33`) records scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload size, but nothing about persistence, so the tension is currently unowned and a future session will re-derive it from scratch.

**F3 — High. The no-persistence choice is under-captured.**

That same mechanism is a load-bearing choice a cold reader would question — holding accepted jobs only in memory, against a stated tenet, on hardware the design itself says takes power blips — and it survives only as a flat assertion in body prose, with no Decisions & Alternatives row. Under *decision capture* — a decision that stays live once landed (a cold reader holding its row and the docs above it would still question or try to reverse it) should have its reasoning on the page — the missing piece is what was given up and what cost was accepted. The remedies for F2 and F3 overlap: if you record the exception (a Decisions row naming the tenet traded against, and why), that also closes F3, so the two can be settled together.

**What I'd recommend.** The remedy is genuinely your call, and there are three shapes, none of which I'd pick for you:

1. **Change the design** — persist the accepted-but-undispatched queue (SQLite already backs the registry, so the store exists) so the tenet holds through a restart. This is a real intent change to the `job-queue` segment, so it should walk the arrow through `/linked-intent-dev` rather than be hand-patched.
2. **Record the exception** — keep the in-memory design, but add a Decisions & Alternatives row to `job-queue-design.md` naming the "job is a promise" tenet as the thing traded against, with the accepted cost (a power blip loses accepted jobs; members resubmit). This is the smallest capture and also resolves F3. If you want the full option space (persist vs. accept-loss vs. periodic snapshot) on the record because other parts of the system schedule around restart behavior, a short decision doc is an option too — but a fuller row is the default, and a decision doc is the exception.
3. **Revise the tenet** — narrow it (e.g., "a submitted job is a promise, except across a service restart") so the intent matches what the design actually delivers.

Which of these do you want to pursue? If it's (1) or (3), that's intent-level work I'd route through the workflow; if it's (2), it's a targeted edit to the design doc's Decisions table that you or the workflow can apply.
