**F2 — High. The queue's in-memory design works against the project's own promise tenet.**

`job-queue-design.md:14` states the queue is held "entirely in process memory," and on startup the service only "rebuilds its picture of the farm by polling each registered printer for the job it is currently running; jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The same LLD's Context (line 10) names "the occasional power blip" as an ordinary operating condition, and the HLD's first tenet is "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (`high-level-design.md:20`), backed by the goal of "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (line 26). Under *tenet cascade* — checking each design mechanism against the HLD's tenets and goals, which rarely share wording — the mechanism defeats the tenet under a condition the design itself expects. Picture the actual failure: a member uploads at 10pm, a blip occurs, and the job silently vanishes; they believe it's queued, come in at 7am, and the printer is empty. That is the one behavior the whole service exists to guarantee, broken in the exact scenario it was built for. LID doesn't pick the remedy for you — the three options are to *change the design* (persist accepted jobs so they survive restart, which would also align the queue with the registry's on-disk store), *record the exception* (a Decisions row naming the promise tenet traded against and why simplicity won — cheap, and consistent with "the service stays small"), or *revise the tenet*. Only the first two keep the walk-away promise; the second is the lightest.

**F3 — High. The restart-loss choice isn't captured in the Decisions & Alternatives table.**

The job-queue LLD's Decisions & Alternatives table (`job-queue-design.md:29-33`) records scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload size — but not the choice to keep the queue in memory and drop undispatched jobs on restart, which lives only as architecture prose. Under *docs carry current intent* (a doc carries present intent, written to be read cold), the cold-reader test is: would someone reading the landed design, with the table and the HLD in hand, still question or want to reverse this choice? Here the answer is clearly yes — it trades durability against the "service stays small" tenet, and a reader would want to know what alternative was weighed and what cost was accepted. Because no row names it, the next session meets the choice cold and either re-derives it or quietly contradicts the tenet. The remedy is the smallest one that settles it: a fuller Decisions row naming the rejected alternative (persist the queue to disk — e.g. SQLite, as the registry already does) and the accepted cost (added complexity/disk vs. job survivability), with the "members resubmit" expectation stated. A decision doc would be overkill here unless the team wants the full durability option space (SQLite vs. append-only log vs. reusing the registry store) argued on the record — the row is normally enough. Note that recording the exception for F2 also closes this finding, since the row would carry the trade; the user may still choose a different remedy for the tenet, in which case this row stands on its own.
