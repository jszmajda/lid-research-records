**F1 — High. The job queue keeps accepted jobs only in process memory, so a restart silently drops them.**

`job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue "is held entirely in process memory," and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The same design's Context (`:10`) names the trigger directly — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." So the failure condition isn't hypothetical; the doc itself describes the machine rebooting. That collides with your HLD's first tenet, "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and with the goal "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (`high-level-design.md:20,26`). The two never share wording — the tenet speaks of promises, the design speaks of memory and restarts — which is exactly the kind of conflict *tenets capture forward-looking preference* (tenets are the tie-breakers for choices no spec anticipated, so a mechanism that quietly defeats one is drift) and *coherence is adjacency* (each level should agree with its neighbor) are meant to catch. Under *docs carry current intent*, there's a second gap: nothing records that the loss-on-restart trade was chosen on purpose. The design simply asserts it. A cold reader — future you, or an agent resuming this segment months from now — can't tell whether the trade was deliberate and priced, or an accident nobody owned, and that ambiguity is what lets the drift survive. The cost of leaving it is direct and compounding: a member who follows the documented workflow (upload in the evening, walk away) can lose their job to a routine update, and there's no spec, test, or decision that would ever flag it. Since the memory choice is what makes the promise impossible to keep, the queue's volatile storage is a design concern in its own right, not just a missing record — F1 folds two findings onto one choice.

The remedy is yours to choose, and the principles deliberately don't pick for you:

- **Change the design** so the promise holds — persist accepted, not-yet-dispatched jobs (the registry already demonstrates a SQLite store fits the mini-PC: `printer-registry-design.md:14`), and add an EARS spec for recovery on startup alongside `QUEUE-004`.
- **Record the exception** — a Decisions & Alternatives row in `job-queue-design.md` naming the tenet traded against and why (e.g., the mini-PC is too modest to persist reliably, and resubmission is accepted as the failure mode). This also closes the decision-capture half of F1; a row here is the smallest capture that settles the question. A full decision doc isn't warranted — this is one trade with an obvious alternative, not a researched option space.
- **Revise the tenet** if "a promise" was never the real intent — restate it as a best-effort lean so the design and the HLD stop disagreeing.

One note if you lean toward *record the exception*: it resolves the tenet conflict and the capture gap on this one choice but leaves the member-facing goal unmet. Whether that's acceptable is a product call, so I'd surface it to whoever owns the "upload and walk away" promise rather than settle it in the design doc alone.
