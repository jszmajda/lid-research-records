**F1 — High. The in-memory queue mechanism works against the HLD's durability tenet and overnight goal under the operating conditions the LLD itself describes.** In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:10-14`, the Context section notes that members upload mostly in the evening for overnight dispatch while the workshop mini-PC *"takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip,"* and the Architecture section specifies that *"the queue is a per-printer-class list of accepted jobs held entirely in process memory"* so *"jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them."* Meanwhile, `docs/high-level-design.md:20-26` sets the first tenet as *"A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away"* and the first goal as *"Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present."* Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — leaf designs should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean, or explicitly record the exception — and *coherence is adjacency* — each level of the arrow agrees with its adjacent level — an unattended overnight reboot drops every queued-but-undispatched job while the member is asleep, defeating the "upload and walk away" promise and the morning-pickup goal. Leaving this contradiction unresolved means future sessions reading the HLD and the LLD get conflicting signals about whether queued jobs are disposable or durable, inviting agents to either quietly add persistence or build user flows that assume jobs never vanish. To resolve this, you have three choices depending on your actual intent: (1) change the design in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (and cascade to `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md`) to persist the queue — for instance in the SQLite file `printer-registry` already uses (`docs/intent/printer-registry/printer-registry-design.md:25`), (2) keep the in-memory design and record the exception in `job-queue-design.md`'s `Decisions & Alternatives` table naming the HLD tenet and goal traded against and why, or (3) revise the tenet and goal in `docs/high-level-design.md` so they only promise durability once a job is dispatched.

**F2 — High.
