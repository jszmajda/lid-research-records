**F1 — High. The in-memory queue in `job-queue` works directly against the HLD's core tenet and overnight-print goal under the LLD's own described operating conditions.** In `docs/high-level-design.md:20`, the first tenet states *"A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away,"* and the primary goal (`docs/high-level-design.md:26`) is *"Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present."* Meanwhile, `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:10` notes in its Context that members upload mostly in the evening, jobs dispatch overnight as printers free up, and the workshop mini-PC *"takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip"* — yet line 14 specifies that the queue is held *"entirely in process memory"* and that *"jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them."* Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* (tenets state which way design nodes should lean when choices arise) and *coherence is adjacency* (each level of the arrow agrees with the level above it), a leaf LLD mechanism that defeats an HLD tenet and goal under normal, anticipated operating conditions is a seam where the arrow disagrees with itself. Leaving that contradiction unresolved means an overnight OS reboot or brief power blip silently drops every waiting evening upload while members are asleep, breaking the exact "upload and walk away" promise the system was built to deliver — and any future session reading both docs cold won't know whether to trust the HLD's promise or the LLD's volatility. You have three ways to resolve this, depending on where your actual intent sits: (1) **change the design** in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (and update `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md`, e.g. `QUEUE-004`) to persist queued jobs across restarts (for instance, in the SQLite database `printer-registry` already uses on the mini-PC); (2) **record the exception** by adding a row to `Decisions & Alternatives` in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` that explicitly names the *"submitted job is a promise"* tenet and overnight-pickup goal being traded against and explains why dropping undispatched jobs on restart is accepted; or (3) **revise the tenet and goal** in `docs/high-level-design.md` to reflect best-effort queuing across restarts if volatile in-memory state is truly what you want.
