**F1 — High. The in-memory queue in `job-queue` conflicts with the HLD's core tenet and overnight-print goal under the operating conditions the LLD itself describes.** In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (`§Context`, line 10), the service runs on a workshop mini-PC that "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip," while `§Architecture` (line 14) specifies that accepted jobs are held "entirely in process memory" so that after a restart, "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" (anchored downstream by `QUEUE-004`). Meanwhile, `docs/high-level-design.md` declares the tenet *"A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away"* (line 20) and the goal *"Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present"* (line 26), with no `Decisions & Alternatives` row or decision doc recording an exception. Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — tenets state which way to lean when choices arise — and *coherence is adjacency* — each level of the arrow agrees with the level above it — leaf designs should either lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals direct or explicitly record the trade-off. Leaving this unresolved means an overnight OS update or power blip silently drops every queued evening job after the first print finishes, defeating the promise that members can upload from home and collect their part in the morning, while future sessions reading the HLD vs. the LLD get contradictory signals on whether accepted jobs are durable. To resolve this, you have three options depending on your actual intent: (1) **change the design** in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` and `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md` so accepted jobs persist across restarts (for example, storing the queue in the same SQLite database that `printer-registry-design.md` already uses on the mini-PC); (2) **record the exception** by adding a row to `## Decisions & Alternatives` in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` that explicitly names the HLD tenet and goal being traded against and why in-memory volatility is accepted; or (3) **revise the tenet and goal** in `docs/high-level-design.md` if best-effort queuing with manual resubmission after restarts is the intended behavior (walking whichever path you choose through `/linked-intent-dev` so the LLD and `QUEUE-004` stay in sync).

**F2 — High. The choice to keep the job queue purely in process memory is stated in architecture prose without a `Decisions & Alternatives` row capturing what was given up and why.** In `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (`§Architecture`, line 14), the doc states that the queue is held in process memory, undispatched jobs are lost on restart, and G-code files are kept on disk under a content hash to make resubmitting cheap, while `## Decisions & Alternatives` (lines 29–33) captures scheduling order, job slugs, and the 64 MB limit, but omits queue persistence entirely. Under *docs carry current intent, written to be read cold* — a doc stands on its own for a reader with no memory of prior chats, and any choice a cold reader would still question or want to reverse belongs in the Decisions table with its rejected alternative and accepted cost — this choice remains live once landed. A cold reader holding `job-queue-design.md` alongside `docs/high-level-design.md` and `docs/intent/printer-registry/printer-registry-design.md` (which already persists printer state in a single SQLite file on the same mini-PC at line 25) will immediately question why the queue isn't persisted in SQLite too, or why disk is used for content-hashed G-code files but not for the queue metadata itself; without the reasoning on the page, a future session is likely to either treat the in-memory queue as an oversight and rewrite it, or Cargo-Cult the limitation without knowing what constraint it was protecting. If you decide to keep the in-memory design rather than switching to persistent storage under F1, try adding a row to `## Decisions & Alternatives` in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` — that is the smallest capture that settles the question — naming the rejected alternative (e.g., persisting the queue in SQLite or on disk), why it was rejected, and the accepted cost (members must resubmit undispatched jobs after an unattended reboot, trading against the HLD's upload-and-walk-away tenet, which also closes F1 if you choose the exception route).
