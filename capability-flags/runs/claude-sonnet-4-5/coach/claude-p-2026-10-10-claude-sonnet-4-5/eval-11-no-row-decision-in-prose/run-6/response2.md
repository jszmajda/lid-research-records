You've surfaced a tension I should have caught in the initial review — this is exactly the kind of tenet cascade issue dimension 19 is designed to detect. Let me walk through it.

**The in-memory queue design works against the HLD's first tenet under the project's own described operating conditions.** The HLD's opening tenet states "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," which sets the expectation that submission creates a durable obligation. The job-queue LLD (§Architecture) says "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them," while the LLD's Context section explicitly notes "the workshop mini-PC... takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Under *tenet cascade* — design nodes should lean the way the HLD says to lean — the mechanism (ephemeral in-memory queue) defeats the tenet (job as promise) under a condition the design itself describes as routine. This matters because the tenet tells future sessions how to break ties when a decision has two defensible answers; if the current design already contradicts it, the tenet actively misleads rather than guides. A member uploading at 8pm for an overnight print would reasonably expect the promise to survive a mini-PC reboot, but the design as specified doesn't deliver that. The Decisions & Alternatives table in the job-queue LLD has no row acknowledging this tradeoff, so a future reader can't tell whether the contradiction is deliberate (accepted for simplicity or performance) or an oversight waiting to be fixed.

**What you could do:** Three remedies, your choice. (1) **Persist the queue** — change the design to write accepted jobs to disk (SQLite alongside the registry, or append-log) so restarts don't lose them; this aligns the mechanism with the tenet. (2) **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row to the job-queue LLD naming the tenet traded against ("promise" tenet), the choice (ephemeral queue for simplicity), and the accepted cost (jobs lost on restart, members resubmit). This documents the tradeoff without changing behavior. (3) **Revise the tenet** — if "upload and walk away" was never meant to survive restarts, soften or replace the tenet to match what the design actually commits to (e.g., "members submit without monitoring, barring infrastructure failure"). Option (2) also closes a decision-capture gap, since the choice currently has no record at all.
