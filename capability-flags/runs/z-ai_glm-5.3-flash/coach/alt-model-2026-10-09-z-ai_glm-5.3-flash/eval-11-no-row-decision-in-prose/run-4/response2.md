**F2 — High. The in-memory queue that loses accepted jobs on restart works against one of your own tenets, and the trade isn't recorded anywhere.** The observation: `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` states the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." Meanwhile the HLD's first tenet (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) says "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the first goal is "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." The two never share wording, which is exactly the shape the *tenets capture forward-looking preference* principle warns about: a future session reading the job-queue design has nothing on the page telling them the durability choice was weighed against the promise tenet, so a well-meaning fix (add SQLite persistence) or a re-derivation (drop it again) can happen without anyone revisiting the trade. And this isn't a rare condition — the design itself says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" (`job-queue-design.md:10`), so restarts under load are an operating condition these docs explicitly describe, not a hypothetical. Left as prose-only fact, this reads as an accident of implementation rather than a decision, and it will keep resurfacing.

There are three ways to resolve a tenet conflict like this, and the choice is yours rather than mine:

1. **Change the design** — persist accepted-but-undispatched jobs (you already persist the G-code files under content hash, so the expensive part is kept; only the queue ordering would need a durable home). This honors the tenet as written.
2. **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row in `job-queue-design.md`, something like: *"Queue durability — accepted jobs lost on restart | Chosen: in-memory only | Rejected: persisting the queue — the overnight window is short, restarts are rare, and resubmission is cheap since files are already content-hashed on disk; accepted cost: a restart between upload and dispatch breaks the 'upload and walk away' tenet for affected members."* Recording the exception also closes the decision-capture side of this finding, since the same row settles *why* the queue is memory-only.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if you conclude the promise tenet overreaches (e.g. soften to "a submitted job survives normal operation"), the HLD tenet should say so, and the design's behavior stops being an exception at all.

My one piece of coaching on the choice: options 2 and 3 both cost one line and take a minute; option 1 is a real design change that would also want a new EARS spec (something like a WHEN-service-restarts-THEN-recover-undispatched-jobs spec) and cascade through the dispatch behavior. If you're unsure, recording the exception first (option 2) is cheap and reversible — and if you later change the design, you mutate the row rather than accumulating history. Also worth noting: if you pick option 1 or the softened tenet, the change touches the HLD and the LLD in the same motion — cascade within a segment is fine to run freely, but this particular edit sits near the HLD/LLD seam, so confirm the tenet wording with yourself (or whoever owns the HLD) before propagating it down.
