**F2 — High. The queue holds accepted jobs only in process memory, and that mechanism works against your own tenet.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` states the design plainly: "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." Your HLD tenet says *"A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away"* — and the Context section of the same LLD names exactly the condition where this bites: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." An OS update at 2am on a queue of evening uploads silently deletes every not-yet-dispatched job; the member walks in the next morning expecting a part that doesn't exist. Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* (tenets are tie-breakers for decisions the specs didn't anticipate — when two defensible answers exist, the tenet tells you which way to lean), this is the case the tenet exists for: the in-memory design and the tenet pull in opposite directions, and no Decisions & Alternatives row records the trade. Left unrecorded, every future session reading the LLD cold will either re-derive the concern from scratch or — worse — treat the in-memory choice as deliberate and let it shape downstream work (retry logic, member notifications) that assumes jobs don't vanish.

This is a tenet-or-goal conflict, so the remedy is your choice, not mine — three options:

1. **Change the design** — persist accepted jobs (e.g., write an accepted-jobs record to the same SQLite file the registry already uses, or even just the G-code path plus metadata, which you partly have already since files are content-hashed on disk). The G-code itself already survives restarts by design; only the intent "this file is queued" is volatile.
2. **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet traded against: "In-memory queue — *A submitted job is a promise* traded against mini-PC disk reliability; accepted cost: an unattended update or power blip loses undelivered jobs and members must resubmit; deemed acceptable because updates are rare and announced." This also closes the decision-capture side of the finding on the same choice. Reasonable if you genuinely think the failure mode is rare and cheap.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if resubmission is genuinely fine for your members, soften the tenet to say so (e.g., "a submitted job is a promise *once dispatch begins*") rather than leaving a tenet the design quietly violates.

My honest read: option 2 is defensible only if "occasional power blip" is truly rare and members tolerate silent loss; if you're at all unsure, option 1 is cheap given you already have SQLite and content-hashed files in the design. Whichever you pick, it should land as a change in `job-queue-design.md` (and a new EARS spec if you go with persistence, e.g. "When the service starts after accepting undelivered jobs, the system SHALL requeue them"), then cascade into tests before code. Happy to work through the persistence design or draft the row with you — your call on the direction.
