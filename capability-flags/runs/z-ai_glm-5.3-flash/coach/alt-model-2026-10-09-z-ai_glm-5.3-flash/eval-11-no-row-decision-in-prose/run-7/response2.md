**F4 — Medium, with a design question attached. The job queue holds accepted jobs only in process memory, and every restart silently drops them.**

The observation sits in one sentence of `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`: the queue is "held entirely in process memory," and "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The HLD's first tenet (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) says "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the Context section of the same LLD names the operating condition that makes this bite — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." So under a condition the docs themselves describe, the mechanism defeats the tenet: a member uploads at 8pm, the box reboots for an OS update at 2am, and the promise is broken with no one there to notice and resubmit. This is a *tenet cascade* finding — a leaf mechanism working against an HLD tenet under a stated condition, where the two share no wording. It's also a *decision-capture* gap: the Durability trade-off appears nowhere in the Decisions & Alternatives table, so a cold reader can't tell whether the loss is a deliberate accepted cost or just something that fell out of the implementation.

Why this matters here specifically: printfarm's whole value proposition is overnight unattended printing. A durability gap doesn't degrade some edge case — it degrades the primary flow, and it does so invisibly (the member finds out the next morning, not at submission time). Left undocumented, every future session touching the queue also can't tell if the in-memory choice is load-bearing or accidental.

The remedy is your choice among three, and I won't pick for you:

1. **Change the design** — persist accepted jobs (you already hash G-code files to disk; persisting a small queue record beside them is the natural move) so the tenet holds across restarts.
2. **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row to `job-queue-design.md` naming the tenet traded against and why in-memory is acceptable (e.g., restarts are rare, resubmission is cheap because files are on disk, staff can see the gap). Recording the exception also closes the decision-capture side of this finding.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if you decide "upload and walk away" only holds between restarts, soften the tenet to say so, so the HLD stops promising more than the design delivers.

One adjacent note while you're in that file: the "job accepted only if the target class has a registered printer" rule (`job-queue-design.md:20`) has no EARS spec behind it — if you change the acceptance or durability design, that's the moment to spec both (finding F3). Whichever remedy you choose, the change is an intent-layer edit and should cascade within the job-queue segment — happy to work through the row wording or the design change with you if you want.
