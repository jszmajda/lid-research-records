Here's the detailed walk-through of **F2 (high)** — the job queue keeping accepted jobs only in memory.

**The observation.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue is "a per-printer-class list of accepted jobs held entirely in process memory," and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." Meanwhile the project's own docs describe exactly the conditions where this bites: the HLD's goal is "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (high-level-design.md:26), the job-queue Context notes the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" (job-queue-design.md:10), and the first tenet is "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (high-level-design.md:20). Notice the three never share wording — that's why the conflict is easy to miss. An unattended OS update restarting the service at 2 a.m. destroys every accepted-but-queued job, and the member who uploaded at 9 p.m. finds nothing printed and never gets told. Under the operating conditions these docs themselves describe, the mechanism works against the tenet.

**The principles.** Two are in play. First, *intent attaches at the lowest node that dominates it* and dimension 19 (tenet cascade): a design mechanism shouldn't defeat an HLD tenet under conditions the docs describe, and when it does, the exception needs a record. Second, *decision capture*: whether or not the in-memory choice was deliberate, a cold reader holding this row and the HLD would absolutely question it or try to reverse it — yet no Decisions & Alternatives row records it. The existing rows (scheduling order, slugs, upload limit) are well-captured; this is the one load-bearing choice that survives only in architecture prose.

**Why it matters.** This is exactly the intent gap LID exists to close: the decision a future session meets cold. Right now the loss-on-restart behavior reads as an implementation accident rather than an accepted cost — the next session touching the queue can't tell whether durability was considered and rejected (e.g., SQLite was already chosen for the registry, so "just persist jobs too" is an obvious-looking reverse) or never thought about. If it sits, either the tenet quietly stops being true, or a future session "fixes" it without weighing what in-memory buys you (no write-path complexity, no staleness between queue state and printer reality after a crash).

**What I'd recommend — your choice, three legitimate remedies:**

1. **Change the design** — persist the accepted-job queue (SQLite is already in the project's toolkit via printer-registry). Most faithful to the tenet, most work.
2. **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row (or a fuller capture) in job-queue-design.md naming the tenet traded against: e.g., *"Queue persistence — in-memory only; trades against 'a submitted job is a promise' under restart; accepted cost: members resubmit after the rare unattended restart; G-code stays on disk so resubmission is cheap."* If you also want member-facing mitigation (a "your job was lost, resubmit" notice), that's a new spec for the file.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if the real intent is "a submitted job is a promise *once printing starts*," soften the tenet to say that.
