**The in-memory queue, read against your own tenet — this one deserves a working session rather than a shrug.**

The design states it plainly: the queue is a per-printer-class list held entirely in process memory, and "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`). The Context section on the same page tells us when that restart will happen — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip," and members upload "mostly in the evening; jobs dispatch overnight." So the loss window is not an edge case: it is precisely the operating condition the service exists for.

That puts the mechanism in tension with your first tenet — *"a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away."* A member uploads at 9pm, an unattended OS update reboots the box at 3am, and in the morning the part doesn't exist and nobody was told. The tenet says upload-and-walk-away; the mechanism quietly adds *unless the mini-PC restarts, in which case you needed to stay*. This is the pattern dimension 19 asks about — a mechanism that works against a tenet under a described operating condition, where the two share no wording. And there's a second layer: the volatility is recorded only as prose in the Architecture section, not as a Decisions & Alternatives row, so a cold reader can't tell whether the loss was a deliberate, priced trade or something nobody examined — *decision capture*.

Worth noting what's already on your side: the G-code files survive on disk under a content hash (`job-queue-design.md:14`), so the expensive part of a job is durable and re-accepting after a restart would be cheap. That makes the persistence option unusually low-cost for this project.

Following the skill's posture on tenet conflicts, this is your choice, with three shapes:

- **Change the design** — persist accepted-job metadata (id, class, priority, file hash) to disk alongside the G-code, and rebuild the queue on startup next to the current poll-for-current-job rebuild (`QUEUE-004`). Small surface, and it would turn "jobs are gone" into "jobs survive."
- **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming tenet 1 as the thing traded against: e.g. *"Queue held in memory; accepted-but-undispatched jobs lost on restart — unattended restarts are rare and jobs are cheap to resubmit; accepted cost: members discover the loss at morning pickup."* If you take this route, also consider whether "members discover the loss at pickup" is actually acceptable against the promise — it's the sharpest edge of the trade.
- **Revise the tenet** — narrow it to something the design honors, e.g. "a submitted job is a promise *unless the farm restarts overnight*." I'd flag that this version reads weaker as a tie-breaker, but it's honest if the trade is deliberate.
