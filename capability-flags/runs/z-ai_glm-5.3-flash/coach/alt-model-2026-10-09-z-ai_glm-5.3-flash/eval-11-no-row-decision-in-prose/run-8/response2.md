**F2 — High. The job queue holds accepted-but-undispatched jobs only in process memory, and a restart loses them.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` §Architecture states the queue is "held entirely in process memory," and the same paragraph acknowledges the consequence directly: "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The Context section one paragraph up says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" — so the loss condition isn't hypothetical; it's an operating condition these docs describe. Under *tenet cascade* (intent attaches at the lowest node that dominates it; cascade within a segment, pause across), a leaf mechanism that works against an HLD tenet needs either to change or to record the exception. Here the mechanism sits in tension with your first tenet — *a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away* — and no Decisions & Alternatives row names the tenet traded against or why. The docs never use the same words ("memory" vs. "promise"), which is exactly the pairing a cold reader has to reconstruct on their own, and a future session might "fix" the durability gap — or defend it — without knowing it was ever considered.

**Why it matters:** the tenet is the promise your web UI makes to members who upload from home in the evening. If a 2 a.m. unattended update restarts the service and a queued-but-not-yet-dispatched job silently evaporates, the member finds out at morning pickup — the precise failure the project exists to prevent. The mitigations nearby (content-hashed G-code on disk so resubmission is cheap) reduce the cost, but don't remove the surprise: nothing in the UI story says "your job may vanish."

**Recommended action — it's your choice, three shapes:**

1. **Change the design** — persist the queue (the registry already uses SQLite; an accepted-jobs table with a state column is the obvious neighbor), and rebuild from it on startup instead of, or alongside, polling printers. This likely touches `QUEUE-004` (which currently specifies rebuild-by-polling only) and may earn a new spec — walk it through `/linked-intent-dev`.
2. **Record the exception** — keep in-memory queues, but add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet traded against: e.g. *"Queue durability | In-memory; accepted-undispatched jobs lost on restart | Accepted cost: members resubmit after a restart — rare (OS updates), and content-hashed files make resubmission one click."* That makes the trade-off visible to every future session and closes the finding.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if "resubmit after a restart" is genuinely acceptable to you, soften the tenet to match (e.g., "a submitted job is a promise, unless the service restarts"). Tenets should describe what you'll actually defend; an overstated one erodes the others.

Options 2 and 3 are one-row edits; option 1 is a real design change and should go through the full workflow rather than be patched directly.
