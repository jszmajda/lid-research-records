**F2 — High. In-memory queueing conflicts with the product’s core promise.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` keeps accepted jobs entirely in process memory and explicitly says undispatched jobs disappear on restart, while the same document notes unattended OS updates and power blips (`:10`). That works against “A submitted job is a promise” (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and the unattended overnight-printing goal (`:26`): after a restart, the member must notice the loss and resubmit rather than simply collect the part. Keeping G-code on disk only makes resubmission cheaper; it does not preserve the accepted job or its queue position. Under *tenet cascade — leaf mechanisms should uphold the HLD’s tenets and goals*, this needs an explicit product decision. You have three coherent remedies: **change the design** to persist accepted jobs and recover them; **record an exception** in the LLD naming the promise/goal being traded away and why; or **revise the HLD** if loss-and-resubmit is genuinely the intended member experience. Recording the exception would also close F3.

**F3 — High. The reasoning behind accepting job loss is not captured.** The LLD’s Decisions & Alternatives table records scheduling, identifiers, and upload limits, but not the much more consequential choice to accept volatile queue state. Under *decision capture — preserve reasoning for choices a cold reader would still question*, a future maintainer cannot tell whether this is a deliberate simplicity trade-off, a temporary bootstrap shortcut, or an accidental omission. That makes it easy either to cement unintended behavior in tests or to reverse a deliberate constraint without understanding its cost. The smallest sufficient remedy is a fuller Decisions row naming the rejected alternative—persistent accepted-job state—and the accepted cost—loss of undispatched work and manual resubmission after restart—along with the reason that trade-off is acceptable. If you instead choose persistence, replace the current restart description and add a row explaining the selected recovery model; a separate decision document is unnecessary unless you want to preserve a substantial comparison of storage and recovery options.

If you choose **durable queueing**, I recommend defining the recovery behavior before implementation: what is persisted at acceptance, whether priority and FIFO positions survive, how jobs already running on printers are reconciled, and how retries avoid duplicate prints. Then express those behaviors as EARS specs and add tests for restart-before-dispatch, restart-during-dispatch, and restart-with-a-job-already-running. If you choose **volatile queueing**, specify how members are informed of lost work and clarify whether staff can recover jobs from the retained G-code.

Once you choose a direction, run `/linked-intent-dev` with that decision so the HLD, LLD, specs, tests, and eventual implementation stay coherent.
