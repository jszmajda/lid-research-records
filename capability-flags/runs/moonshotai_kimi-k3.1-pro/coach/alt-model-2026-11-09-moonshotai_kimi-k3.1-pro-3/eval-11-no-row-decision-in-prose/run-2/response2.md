**F2 — High. The restart behavior changes the user-facing meaning of “accepted.”** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:10-16` explicitly anticipates unattended OS updates and power blips, but keeps accepted jobs entirely in process memory and requires members to resubmit them after a restart. That conflicts with the HLD’s “A submitted job is a promise” tenet (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and its goal of overnight printing without a member present (`:26`). `QUEUE-004` only rebuilds printer state; it does not restore pending work. Under *tenet cascade* — component mechanisms should uphold the parent design’s stated preferences or record an exception — this needs an explicit product decision. Otherwise, a routine restart can silently turn a successful upload into no print, precisely when the member has walked away and expects collection the next morning.

**F3 — High. The durability tradeoff is not captured as a decision.** The same LLD records rationale and accepted costs for scheduling, identifiers, and upload size, but its Decisions & Alternatives table says nothing about why accepted-job durability was traded away. Under *decision capture* — a cold reader should be able to reconstruct a choice they would still question — this looks like a load-bearing gap rather than documentation polish. A future maintainer cannot tell whether losing jobs was deliberate, temporary, or simply overlooked, making it harder to change the design safely. A fuller table row is sufficient here; this does not yet warrant a separate decision document.

You have three legitimate ways to resolve both findings:

1. **Change the design: make acceptance durable.** Persist accepted-job metadata and queue ordering, with the G-code remaining content-addressed on disk. A small SQLite-backed queue is a natural candidate given the existing mini-PC constraint. On startup, restore undispatched jobs and reconcile running jobs with the printers. You would also need intent for the hardest edge: a restart while dispatch is in progress, where the printer may or may not have accepted the job, so recovery does not accidentally print it twice. Add EARS and tests for restart before dispatch, restart during uncertain dispatch, and restoration of queue order.

2. **Keep the in-memory design and record an exception.** Add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet and overnight-printing goal being traded against, the rejected durable alternative, why simplicity wins, and the accepted cost: accepted jobs can disappear on restart. Then specify the user-visible recovery behavior—at minimum, how members learn their jobs were lost and what they must resubmit. Recording the exception would also close F3.

3. **Revise the HLD promise.** If best-effort queuing before dispatch is the intended product, soften “A submitted job is a promise” and adjust the unattended overnight goal so they no longer imply durable acceptance. Keep the job-queue LLD and EARS explicit about resubmission and notification. Capture that choice in the same decisions row.

My recommendation is to settle that contract before implementation rather than letting the current in-memory sentence define it accidentally. If you choose durable acceptance, run `/linked-intent-dev` with “persist accepted jobs and restore them safely after restart” so the LLD, EARS, tests, and eventual code change together.

Which guarantee do you want printfarm to make: **accepted jobs survive restarts**, or **acceptance is best-effort until dispatch**?
