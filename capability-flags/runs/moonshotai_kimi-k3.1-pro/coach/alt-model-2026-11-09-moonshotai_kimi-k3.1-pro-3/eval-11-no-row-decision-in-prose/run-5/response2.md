**F1 — High. The queue’s restart behavior conflicts with the HLD’s promise.** `job-queue-design.md:14` keeps accepted jobs entirely in process memory and says undispatched jobs are lost on restart, while the HLD says “A submitted job is a promise” (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and promises unattended overnight printing. The LLD also explicitly expects unattended updates and power blips (`job-queue-design.md:10`), so this is not merely a theoretical edge case. Under *tenet cascade — component mechanisms should not defeat project-level commitments without a recorded exception*, the current design leaves members unable to reliably “upload and walk away.” `QUEUE-004` specifies rebuilding state from printers, but no spec defines what members should expect for accepted work that disappears. Resolving this before implementation will prevent recovery semantics and member-facing expectations from being built around an unintended contradiction.

**F4 — Medium. The reasoning behind that trade-off is not captured.** The Decisions & Alternatives table records FIFO scheduling, identifiers, and upload size, but not the more consequential choice to sacrifice queued jobs for an in-memory design. Under *decision capture — retain the reasoning for choices a cold reader would question*, a future maintainer cannot tell whether losing accepted jobs is deliberate, temporary, or simply an oversight, nor what alternatives and operating costs were considered. A short table row should name the chosen storage model, rejected alternative, rationale, and accepted cost; a separate decision document is unnecessary unless you want to research and preserve a broader durability-options analysis.

**There are three coherent ways to resolve the tenet conflict; the choice is yours:**

- **Change the design:** persist accepted jobs and their queue metadata, then restore undispatched work on startup. This most directly supports the existing promise. You would need to specify recovery ordering, dispatch de-duplication, and retention of the uploaded G-code—not just add a database.
- **Keep the design and record an exception:** retain the in-memory queue, but add a Decisions row explicitly naming the submitted-job promise and unattended-printing goal being traded against mini-PC simplicity, together with the accepted cost. The member-facing resubmission behavior should also become explicit in the LLD and specs. This row would also close F4.
- **Revise the HLD commitment:** narrow the tenet and goal to state what is actually promised across restarts—for example, retention of uploaded files but not accepted queue entries. Keep an accompanying decision row explaining why the weaker guarantee is appropriate; revising the tenet alone would not close F4.

Once you choose a direction, update the affected HLD/LLD intent first, then revise `QUEUE-004` and add recovery and member-expectation specs before tests and implementation. `/linked-intent-dev` can walk that chosen change through the full workflow.
