**F2 — High. The restart behavior conflicts with the service’s central promise.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` says accepted jobs live entirely in process memory, accepted-but-undispatched jobs disappear on restart, and members must resubmit them. The HLD instead says “A submitted job is a promise” and aims for evening upload, overnight printing, and morning pickup without the member being present (`docs/high-level-design.md:20,26`). Under *tenet cascade* — component mechanisms should honor the project’s declared preferences or explicitly record an exception — an unattended OS update or power blip can therefore defeat the exact workflow the service exists to provide. I recommend resolving this deliberately in one of three ways: **change the design** to persist accepted jobs and reconstruct their queue state after restart; **record the exception** in the queue’s Decisions & Alternatives table, naming the promise and unattended-printing goal being traded against and why losing those jobs is acceptable; or **revise the HLD tenet and goal** if resubmission is genuinely the intended product behavior. The choice is yours, but leaving the documents as they are gives future implementers two incompatible definitions of correct behavior.

**F4 — Medium. The durability trade-off is not captured as a decision.** The queue’s decision table explains FIFO scheduling, recyclable job slugs, and the upload limit, but not why in-memory-only accepted jobs were chosen over durable storage (`job-queue-design.md:27-33`). Under *decision capture* — preserve reasoning for choices a cold reader would still question or consider reversing — a future maintainer cannot tell whether job loss is a deliberate simplicity trade-off or an accidental limitation that should be fixed. The smallest remedy is a row naming the durable alternative, the reason it was rejected, and the accepted cost, including who discovers the loss and resubmits. If you choose to record an exception for F2, that same row can close this finding too; if you instead adopt persistence, the design and specs should describe recovery semantics rather than preserve a rationale for behavior you no longer intend.

Once you choose the intended behavior, run `/linked-intent-dev` with that decision so the HLD or LLD, EARS specs, tests, and eventual implementation are updated coherently.
