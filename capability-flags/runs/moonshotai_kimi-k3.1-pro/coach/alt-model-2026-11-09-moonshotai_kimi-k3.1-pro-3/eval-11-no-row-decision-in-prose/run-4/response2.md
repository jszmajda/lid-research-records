**F2 — High. The queue’s restart behavior works against the HLD’s central promise.** `docs/high-level-design.md:20` says, “A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away,” while `job-queue-design.md:10` explicitly anticipates unattended OS updates and power blips. Despite that, `job-queue-design.md:14` keeps accepted jobs entirely in process memory and says undispatched jobs are gone after a restart, requiring members to resubmit them. `QUEUE-004` reinforces this by rebuilding only printer state, not queued jobs. Under *tenet cascade — component mechanisms should uphold the HLD’s tenets and goals*, this is a direct conflict rather than merely an implementation cost: a restart can silently withdraw the promise after the member has walked away. Resolving it will give the eventual recovery specs and tests one unambiguous behavior to protect.

You have three legitimate remedies, depending on the intent you want:

- **Change the design:** persist accepted jobs and restore undispatched work during startup. This best preserves the current tenet, but adds storage, recovery, and consistency responsibilities.
- **Keep the design and record an exception:** add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the promise being traded against, why in-memory operation is preferred, and the accepted cost that members must resubmit after a restart.
- **Revise the tenet:** narrow the promise so it matches the intended member experience—for example, distinguishing retained uploaded files from durable queue acceptance.

The coach should not choose that product trade-off for you. If you choose durable acceptance, run `/linked-intent-dev` with that change so the HLD/LLD, recovery specs, tests, and implementation evolve together.
