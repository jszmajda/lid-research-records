**F1 — High. The queue’s restart behavior defeats the promise made by the HLD.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:10-14` explicitly anticipates unattended OS updates and power blips, then says accepted-but-undispatched jobs are lost and members must resubmit them. That conflicts with “A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away” (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and the overnight-printing goal (`:26`). Under *tenet cascade — component mechanisms should uphold the project’s declared preferences and goals*, the problem is not in-memory storage by itself; it is the member-visible loss of work after the service has accepted it. Someone could upload successfully, go home, and discover the next day that nothing printed and their job no longer exists. Leaving this unresolved also gives future implementation sessions two contradictory sources of intent to follow.

**F4 — Medium. The durability tradeoff is not recorded as a decision.** The LLD’s Decisions & Alternatives table records scheduling, identifiers, and upload size, but not the choice to discard accepted jobs on restart. Under *decision capture — preserve rationale that a cold reader would still question*, this choice needs an explicit record because a reader of the landed design would reasonably want to reverse it. The table should identify the rejected alternative, such as durable queue storage, and state the accepted cost and why it is worthwhile; otherwise, future sessions may either accidentally preserve the limitation or “fix” it without understanding the original constraints.

### Your options

There are three legitimate resolutions:

1. **Change the design to make acceptance durable.** Persist an accepted job before acknowledging acceptance, restore undispatched jobs during startup, and define how restored state reconciles with jobs printers report as active. The EARS would then need to cover restart recovery and reconciliation, followed by tests and implementation.
2. **Keep the volatile queue and record the exception.** Add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet and overnight goal being traded against, the simplicity gained, and the accepted cost that members must resubmit after a restart. Also specify the member-facing behavior so loss and resubmission are not silent. Recording the exception would close both F1 and F4.
3. **Revise the HLD’s promise.** Narrow the submission tenet—and the overnight goal if necessary—to describe a best-effort queue rather than durable acceptance. This is appropriate only if that is genuinely the service you want members to experience; the upload UI and specs should communicate the same limitation.

Once you choose the intended behavior, run `/linked-intent-dev` with that decision so the change cascades deliberately through the HLD or LLD, EARS, tests, and code. Which promise do you want printfarm to make: **durable acceptance**, or **best-effort queueing with explicit resubmission**?
