**F1 — High. The restart design breaks the accepted-job promise.** The HLD says “a submitted job is a promise” and targets “evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup” (`docs/high-level-design.md:20,26`), but the queue LLD says accepted jobs live entirely in process memory and undispatched jobs “are gone” after restart (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`). Keeping G-code on disk does not preserve the job’s acceptance, queue position, priority, or printer class. A power blip after upload can therefore require a member—who reasonably walked away—to resubmit before anything can print. Under *tenet cascade* — lower-level mechanisms should uphold the HLD’s preferences and goals — this conflict matters because later specs and tests could entrench job loss as intended behavior rather than expose the contradiction.

I recommend resolving the product choice before adding implementation or tests. You have three legitimate options:

1. **Change the design to preserve accepted jobs.** Persist enough queue metadata to restore accepted, undispatched jobs after restart, and make acceptance durable before reporting success. On startup, reconcile restored jobs with each printer’s current job so recovery does not dispatch duplicates. This retains the walk-away promise while accepting modest storage and recovery complexity.
2. **Keep volatile queues and record an explicit exception.** Add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the walk-away tenet and overnight goal being traded against, why simplicity wins, and the accepted cost of lost work. The member-facing behavior should then make that limitation clear, and EARS should specify loss and recovery expectations rather than leaving them as prose.
3. **Revise the HLD promise.** If printfarm is intentionally best-effort, weaken “a submitted job is a promise” and the unattended overnight goal to describe the actual guarantee, then cascade that change through the queue LLD and specs.

Whichever direction you choose, carry it through `/linked-intent-dev` rather than patching only the LLD. The downstream specs should cover restart after acceptance but before dispatch, restoration or loss of queue position, reconciliation of jobs already running on printers, and what the member is told. Those tests can then precede the implementation.
