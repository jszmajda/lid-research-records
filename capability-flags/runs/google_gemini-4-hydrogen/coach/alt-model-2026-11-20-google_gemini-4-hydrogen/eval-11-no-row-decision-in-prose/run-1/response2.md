**F1 — High. The in-memory queue in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` works against the HLD's first tenet and first goal under an operating condition the LLD itself describes.** `job-queue-design.md` (Context and Architecture, lines 10–14) notes that the workshop mini-PC takes "unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip," yet holds accepted, undispatched jobs solely in process memory so that after a restart they are gone and "members resubmit them." That directly undercuts `docs/high-level-design.md` Tenet 1 ("A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away") and Goal 1 ("Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present"), with no exception recorded anywhere in the design. Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — HLD tenets act as tie-breakers that downstream designs follow unless an explicit trade-off is documented — leaving this tension unspoken means future sessions implementing `job-queue` cannot tell whether dropping overnight jobs on reboot is an intentional trade-off or an accidental contradiction. You have three ways to resolve this, depending on what you want the system to promise: change `job-queue-design.md` so accepted jobs survive restarts (for instance, persisting the queue alongside the registry's SQLite store or on disk), keep the in-memory design and record an explicit exception in `job-queue-design.md` `## Decisions & Alternatives` naming Tenet 1 and Goal 1 as what was traded against and why, or revise Tenet 1 in `docs/high-level-design.md` if best-effort delivery across reboots is actually what you want.

Under *docs carry current intent* — design docs stand alone for a cold reader and capture live rationale in Decisions & Alternatives rather than leaving it in past conversations — an unrecorded choice that looks surprising from the landed docs invites future sessions to second-guess or quietly reverse it. Adding a Decisions row in `job-queue-design.md` naming the rejected alternative (such as persisting the queue in SQLite) and the accepted cost settles the question; if you choose to resolve F1 by recording an exception to Tenet 1 in that Decisions row, that single row closes both findings, whereas if you choose to persist the queue instead, the row can record that persistence choice.
