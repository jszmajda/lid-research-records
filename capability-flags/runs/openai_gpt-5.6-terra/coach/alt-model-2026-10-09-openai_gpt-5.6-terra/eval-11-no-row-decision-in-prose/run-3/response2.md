**In-memory queueing conflicts with the product promise.** The queue design says accepted, undispatched jobs live only in process memory and “are gone” after a restart (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`). Its context also explicitly anticipates unattended OS updates and power blips (`:10`). That sits against the HLD’s tenet that a submitted job is a promise and members can upload then walk away (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`), as well as the goal of evening upload and unattended overnight printing (`:26`). Under *tenet cascade* — leaf mechanisms should follow the HLD’s stated preferences — a routine restart could silently turn an accepted overnight submission into a missing job, exactly when the member is not present to recover.

There is also an intent-recording gap. The decision table explains the scheduling policy, IDs, and upload cap, but not the durability tradeoff. Under *docs carry current intent* — live choices and their costs should be understandable cold — a future reader cannot tell whether loss on restart is an intentional simplicity boundary, an unexamined implementation shortcut, or merely temporary. That ambiguity is especially consequential because “keep the service small” is a competing HLD tenet.

I would not choose the product tradeoff for you. The appropriate routes are:

1. **Change the design:** persist accepted job metadata and its G-code reference in SQLite, then restore undispatched jobs on startup. This most directly preserves the upload-and-walk-away promise, and the existing SQLite registry makes it a plausible small-system choice.
2. **Keep in-memory queueing, but record the exception:** add a fuller `job-queue` Decisions row that names the “submitted job is a promise” tenet being traded off, the alternative of durable queue storage, why operational simplicity currently wins, and the accepted member-facing cost. This resolves both the tenet-cascade and decision-capture findings, but makes the product limitation explicit.
3. **Revise the HLD tenet or goal:** if the actual promise is only that uploads are accepted while the service stays running, narrow the HLD language so it does not imply durable submission through planned or unplanned restarts.

If you want unattended overnight operation to be reliable, I would start a `/linked-intent-dev` change for durable queued-job recovery. It can then walk the decision through the LLD, add EARS for persistence and restart recovery, write tests first, and implement it without guessing at the intended failure semantics.
