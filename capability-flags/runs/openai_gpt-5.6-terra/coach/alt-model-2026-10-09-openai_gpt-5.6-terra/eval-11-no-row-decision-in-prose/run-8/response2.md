**F2 — High. The queue’s restart policy conflicts with the service promise.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:10-14` explicitly describes unattended OS updates and power blips, then says accepted-but-undispatched jobs are held only in memory and “are gone” after restart; `docs/high-level-design.md:20-22` calls a submitted job “a promise” so members can upload and walk away. Under *tenet cascade* — leaf-level mechanisms should carry out the HLD’s stated preferences — these positions pull in opposite directions precisely under an operating condition the LLD anticipates. Leaving the conflict implicit means an implementation can plausibly discard a member’s evening upload after a routine restart while still appearing to follow the queue design; the most important guarantee becomes unreliable when nobody is present to notice.

There are three valid directions, and the appropriate one is a product decision rather than something I should choose for you:

- **Change the design:** persist each accepted job’s metadata, priority, queue position, and reference to its stored G-code, then reconstruct undispatched queues at startup. This best honors the current “upload and walk away” tenet. The new behavior should be captured in the `QUEUE` EARS specs before implementation.
- **Record an explicit exception:** keep the in-memory queue, but expand the `job-queue` Decisions & Alternatives row to say that low operational complexity is being chosen over durability, name the submitted-job tenet being traded off, and state why resubmission is acceptable. This makes the promise intentionally narrower rather than accidentally unreliable.
- **Revise the tenet:** if “submitted job” is intended to mean only “accepted during this running process,” make that limitation explicit in the HLD. This is only coherent if members can realistically know whether a job survived overnight.

I would start `/linked-intent-dev` with the chosen guarantee, because it can walk the change from HLD/LLD through EARS and tests before any persistence or recovery code is written. Which guarantee do you want members to receive after a restart: durable queued jobs, or explicitly best-effort submission?
