**In-memory accepted jobs conflict with the project’s promise.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` says the per-class queue exists only in process memory and that accepted-but-undispatched jobs disappear after a restart. That directly conflicts with the HLD’s tenet, “A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away” (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and its overnight-print goal. An unattended OS update or power interruption can silently erase an evening’s submissions, leaving a member without a print the next morning despite having received acceptance. Under *tenet cascade* — leaf-level mechanisms should implement the project’s stated preferences — the queue design needs an explicit resolution rather than leaving that contradiction implicit.

You have three valid directions, and the appropriate one depends on what “accepted” should mean to this makerspace:

1. **Change the design to persist accepted jobs.** Store job metadata and queue order durably alongside the already persisted G-code content, restore pending jobs at startup, and reconcile any in-flight jobs against printer status. This best fulfills the submission promise. It adds a small persistence and recovery design, but that cost appears compatible with the existing SQLite registry and “small service” tenet.

2. **Keep the ephemeral queue, but record an explicit exception.** Update the job-queue Decisions & Alternatives table to name the HLD tenet being traded off, explain why avoiding durable queue state is worth that risk, and state the accepted user-facing consequence. This makes the limitation intentional and visible to future readers rather than an accidental mismatch. It also calls for a spec that tells members what acceptance means after a service restart, likely including notification or a queue-status warning.

3. **Revise the HLD tenet.** If “upload and walk away” was only meant to remove the need to stand at the printer, not to promise durability across service failure, narrow the tenet to say so. This is coherent only if that lower guarantee is genuinely what the makerspace wants to offer.

For the first direction, I would start `/linked-intent-dev` with the desired behavior, such as: “Persist accepted print jobs and recover pending queues after service restart.” That workflow can establish the recovery boundary, failure semantics, EARS coverage, tests, and implementation in arrow order.
