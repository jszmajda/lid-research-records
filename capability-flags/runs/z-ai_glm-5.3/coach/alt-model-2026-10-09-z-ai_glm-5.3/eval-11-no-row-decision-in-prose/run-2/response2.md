**F2 — High. The queue keeps accepted jobs only in memory, and that defeats the HLD's own promise tenet under conditions the LLD itself describes.** `job-queue-design.md` says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the same doc's Context notes the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Put those next to the HLD's tenet "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" and its goal "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present," and the conflict is exact, not hypothetical: the restarts the Context predicts will land overnight, unattended, precisely when the member isn't there to resubmit. Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — a tenet is the tie-breaker a future session leans on when no spec covers the choice — this is the tenet-cascade case the lens exists to catch: the tenet and the mechanism share no wording, so nothing flagged the tension when the LLD was written. The cost of leaving it unresolved isn't just one lost batch of jobs; it's that the written intent now contradicts itself, and whichever one a future session reads first silently wins the argument.

**F4 — Medium. The persistence choice has no Decisions & Alternatives row, so a cold reader can't reconstruct what was given up.** The Decisions table in `job-queue-design.md` records scheduling order, job identifiers, and the upload limit — but not the most consequential choice in the doc: where accepted jobs live before dispatch. Under *docs carry current intent, written to be read cold* — a doc must stand on its own, without the conversation that produced it — a reader holding the promise tenet, the power-blip context, *and* the SQLite file already in `printer-registry-design.md` would naturally ask "why not persist the queue too?" and find no answer. The mechanism's consequence is stated plainly (jobs are gone on restart), but the *choice* — in-memory over persisted, and why — survives only in whatever conversation produced it. This matters because that unrecorded "why" is exactly what a future session re-derives from scratch when it touches the queue, and it may derive the opposite answer. The good news: this is the smallest kind of repair — one fuller row.

**What I'd recommend.** There are three ways to resolve the tenet conflict, and that choice is yours, not mine:

1. **Change the design** — e.g., persist the accepted-queue (the registry's SQLite file, or a sibling file, would fit the "service stays small" tenet too). This is a design change walked forward via `/linked-intent-dev` — it would ripple into the LLD, likely a new spec for restart recovery (which also closes part of F3's restart-loss gap), and eventually code.
2. **Record the exception** — keep in-memory, and add a Decisions row that names the tenet traded against ("a submitted job is a promise") and why the trade is acceptable. This is the smallest capture, and it closes F4 in the same stroke. The row would carry the rejected alternative (persisting the queue) and the accepted cost (overnight job loss on unplanned restarts).
3. **Revise the tenet** — if "upload and walk away" is aspirational rather than a promise, soften the tenet to say so; the LLD then stands as written. Worth considering only if the makerspace would genuinely shrug at losing overnight jobs.

If you ask my read: the tenet and the goal read as the product's whole point, which is why I'd want either option 1 or a deliberate, recorded option 2 — but which one depends on how much you care about the morning-pickup experience versus keeping the service minimal, and that's your call.
