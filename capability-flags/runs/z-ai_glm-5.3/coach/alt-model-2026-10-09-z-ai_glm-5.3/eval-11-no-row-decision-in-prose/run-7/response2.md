Here's the full picture. Two findings share one root — the choice to hold accepted jobs only in process memory — so I'll walk them together.

**F1 — High. The in-memory queue works against Tenet 1 under conditions the docs themselves describe.** `job-queue-design.md:14` states the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the Context at line 10 says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Hold that against the HLD: Tenet 1 says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and Goal 1 is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." The collision is temporal: the queue is *fullest* of accepted-undispatched jobs overnight (evening upload peak, printers still working through the backlog), which is precisely when nobody is there to resubmit. A 2am power blip silently empties the queue, and the member arrives to no part and no explanation. Under *tenet cascade* — leaf designs should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean — this is the exact failure mode tenets exist to prevent: the component's central design choice contradicts the project's own promise, and because the two share no wording ("in process memory" vs "a promise"), no grep would ever surface it. A future session reading the LLD alone would take in-memory as settled intent and never notice the HLD-level promise it breaks.
