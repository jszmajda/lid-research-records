**F1 — High. The restart-loss mechanism works against the HLD's own promise tenet, in the exact window the HLD promises to cover.** The HLD's first tenet is "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and its first goal is "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." The job-queue LLD's Architecture (`job-queue-design.md:14`) holds the queue "entirely in process memory" and says "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the same doc's Context (`:10`) names the cause outright: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Under *tenet cascade* — the design nodes should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean — this mechanism leans the other way: a power blip at 2am lands precisely in the promised overnight window, silently voids the promise, and the member discovers it at morning pickup. The conflict is invisible from either doc alone — the tenet and the mechanism share no wording — which is exactly why this class of drift compounds: every future session will read the LLD as authoritative and never think to check it against the tenet. If the lossy choice is what you actually want, it needs to be recorded as a deliberate exception; if it isn't, this is the moment to fix it, before code hardens the design in place.

**F3 — Medium. The durability decision is under-captured, and the doc contradicts itself about it.** Under *decision capture* (*docs carry current intent* — a choice a cold reader would still question should reconstruct what was given up and what cost was accepted), the in-memory-queue choice is the one row a cold reader will immediately push on — especially given the asymmetry: printer-registry persists in SQLite with the rationale "fits the mini-PC," while job-queue, on the same mini-PC, doesn't. Two details make the gap sharper: the job-ID Decisions row says "slugs recycle after 90 days," which implies a persistent job-record store the design never builds anywhere; and nothing explains how a member even *learns* their job vanished, or how resubmitting "the same file" (cheap via the content hash) restores their queue position versus starting over. The consequence of leaving it loose is that the first implementation session resolves these questions itself — from its own distribution, not yours — and whatever it picks becomes the frozen intent.
