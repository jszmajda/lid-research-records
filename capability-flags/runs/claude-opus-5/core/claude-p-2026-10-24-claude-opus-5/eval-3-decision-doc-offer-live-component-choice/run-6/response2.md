Written: `docs/intent/record-store/decisions/concurrent-visit-edits.md`, linked from the LLD's Decisions row and References.

**Structure.** Context states the problem from the domain — shared tablets, 48-hour offline windows, a weekly partner CSV, and the fact that nothing in the field names distinguishes an unrelated edit from a scientifically coupled one. Then one gate, six weighted criteria, three options, and the selection.

The gate is *no submitted edit is discarded without a human act*, inherited from the HLD tenet. It's there because it actually eliminates an option: I included **most-recent-submission-wins** as a third option, since it's effectively the behavior being replaced, and the gate kills it — retaining a superseded submission in an audit log doesn't satisfy the tenet, it just documents the current failure better.

**Why we'd still consider field-aware merge.** It's written in its advocate's voice and it wins outright on three of the six criteria — review workload (the major one), export completeness, and submission size. Its weakness is stated as the specific mechanism, not a vague risk: *disjoint fields are not the same as unrelated observations*. A worker revising a species identification and another adjusting that species' cover estimate from the same base produce changes the rules see as separable, and the combined record reads as valid. No rule set expressible over field names catches it. Selection says plainly that the choice is taken against the lean of *prefer workflows two coordinators can operate without a dedicated systems administrator* — accepting the queue — and for inspectability and the non-goal of inferring scientific intent. The deciding argument is asymmetry of recovery: a contested visit waits a week; an assembled record is undetectable.

There's a **what would reverse this** paragraph, and it's deliberately not "if the queue gets long" — measured volume alone isn't enough, because reversing means accepting automatic combination of coupled edits. The condition to watch is whether the team gains someone to own merge rules.

**What the tablet and export work has to accommodate** is in Selection as cascades, and the LLD carries the contract-level version of the same obligations:

- **tablet-sync** — retain a base revision ID on every draft and pending submission so an edit made offline still names where it came from two days later; submit complete field sets, not change lists; treat `accepted_contested` as a *success* and say so to a worker who has no connectivity and nobody to ask; never assume its own submission became the current state; handle a pre-resolution revision it still holds going non-current.
- **review-export** — a contested-visit queue as first-class work rather than an error list; a comparison view over the contested response and its server-computed divergence point, showing each competing whole visit against the shared baseline; both resolution forms including composition; send the expected current-revision set so a stale resolution fails; hold contested visits out of the CSV, which makes queue age before Friday something that node has to surface.

Also foreclosed and stated: no field-level provenance. Provenance is whole-revision, so a question about one value is answered by naming the revision and the coordinator.
