The decision doc is written at `docs/intent/record-store/decisions/concurrent-visit-edits.md`, owned by `record-store`, and linked from the LLD's Decisions row.

**Context** sets the stakes from the domain: twelve shared tablets, 48-hour offline windows, evening note comparison producing two edits from one starting state, and a consumer (the science partner) who cannot audit what they receive. The failure mode named explicitly is that a wrong combined record isn't a visible error — it's a plausible record no observer submitted.

**Decision elements.** One inherited gate, from the tenet about not discarding a submitted edit, which eliminates arrival-order current state — today's spreadsheet behaviour. Nothing eliminates either of the two real contenders, so the rest are weighted: integrity of the stored record and ongoing maintenance burden as major, then review workload, export readiness, explainability, and worker-visible correction latency as moderate, with tablet storage cost as minor. Preserving original submissions is recorded as background rather than a criterion, since every option does it and it can't drive selection.

I probed for criteria cutting against the choice, and **worker-visible correction latency** survived — a trivial typo correction that collides with any other edit waits for coordinator attention, where merge would land it immediately. It's in the doc as a moderate criterion, marked as recorded because it cuts against the selection.

**Three options**, each argued as its advocate would. Merge gets its full strength: the specific rules (scalars take the changed side, observer lists as sets, species entries per key), field-level provenance, strong on review volume, export readiness, and latency, and explicitly not refuted by a manual-approval requirement. Its two weak verdicts are on the major criteria — the identification-plus-cover-estimate example for integrity, and the rules as a standing artifact needing re-reasoning each time the survey form changes, with failures today's tests wouldn't catch.

**Selection** states the honest trade rather than reshaping it: merge wins four of seven criteria, but they're all paid in coordinator hours, which are recoverable where a fabricated observation is not. It names the two tenets the decision turns on and the inference non-goal that makes field-name disjointness insufficient rather than merely imperfect.

**Why merge stays worth considering** is its own passage, with three specific conditions that would reopen it: review volume proving unsustainable at season peak, a declared set of fields with no scientific relationship to any other (observer names and notes being the candidates), or a scientific reviewer joining who can own the rules. It says the narrow version — merge a declared safe-field set, diverge on everything else — is the one to revisit first, because it keeps the integrity property for the fields that carry science.

**Downstream obligations** spell out both nodes. tablet-sync: base revision ID retained beside every draft across restarts and multi-day disconnection, sent with each submission; the two acceptance outcomes distinguishable to the worker; editing an already-diverged visit means adding another head and the interface has to say so; an unrecognised-base rejection is handled, not blindly retried. review-export: a comparison view over competing heads and their divergence point, a resolution action carrying every current head, an export set restricted to settled visits, comparison between complete visits rather than fields, and the coordinator's resolution appearing as an authorship event in export provenance.
