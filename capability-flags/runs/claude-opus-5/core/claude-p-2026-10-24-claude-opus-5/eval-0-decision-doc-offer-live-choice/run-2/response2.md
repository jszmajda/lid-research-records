Written up as a project-level decision doc, since the HLD owns the choice.

**New: `docs/decisions/change-delivery-transport.md`** (`node: high-level-design`)

- **Context** — the question stated from the domain: committed revisions exist and have to reach watching browsers; the answer shapes the change store, delivery, and browser sync simultaneously, and getting it wrong costs a rewrite of all three contracts. Carries the operating envelope (40 markets, 800 browsers, 20 edits/sec bursts, 15-second freshness, 20-minute recovery, seven-day retention) so a cold reader can re-run the judgment against the numbers rather than take the conclusion on faith.
- **Decision Elements** — no gate: the freshness goal, the recovery goal, and the one-authoritative-revision tenet are satisfiable under all three options, so they're recorded as background, and the "self-hosted and managed infrastructure are both permitted" tenet means a provider wasn't excluded on principle either. The decision runs on six weighted criteria: update latency and delivery-path independence (*major*), then operational burden, recurring cost, failure visibility/catch-up correctness, and burst tolerance (*moderate*). Failure visibility is tied explicitly to the connectivity-failures-must-be-visible tenet.
- **Options in the Domain** — each of the three described as its advocate would put it, then a verdict-led bullet per criterion. Two things surfaced here that weren't in the original sketch and that cut *against* the choice we made, so they're on the page: polling's **burst tolerance** is actually a strength (a fixed read interval smooths a 20-edit burst, whereas push multiplies it by the audience), and the hosted option's coupling is **semantic as well as commercial** — its subscription model would have become browser-sync's recovery design.
- **Selection** — self-hosted WebSockets wins because it's the only option strong on both major criteria. It states plainly that the decision is taken *against* its own weak criterion (operational burden, where it loses to both alternatives), notes that the cost is partly repaid by traffic tracking changes rather than browsers, and then hands the two unsettled criteria down as named obligations: delivery sizes fan-out for 20 commits/sec × 800 sessions, and the visibility tenet becomes concrete owed work in all three nodes (liveness signal and visible capacity refusal in delivery; disconnection visibility and catch-up in browser sync; a replay window covering 20 minutes in the change store). It closes by noting polling could later be added over the same revision history for constrained clients, so nothing is permanently foreclosed.

**`docs/high-level-design.md`** — the Key Design Decisions entry is now the short form (choice, both rejections in one line, the trade) pointing at the doc; the Approach section's accepted-cost paragraph links it too. The Architecture and Goals edits from the last turn are unchanged.
