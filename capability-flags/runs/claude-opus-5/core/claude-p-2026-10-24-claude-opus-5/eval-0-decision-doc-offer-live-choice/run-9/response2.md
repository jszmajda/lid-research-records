Written: `docs/decisions/change-delivery-route.md`, linked from the HLD's Key Design Decisions entry, which is now a short pointer rather than a duplicate of the reasoning.

The doc is built so a cold reader can re-run the judgment, not just learn the outcome:

- **Context** — why the question exists from the domain (it sizes all three unwritten nodes), the scale and freshness envelope, and the note that nothing eliminates any route: all three meet 15 seconds, and the data-sensitivity tenet keeps a hosted provider legitimately in contention rather than ruled out.
- **Decision Elements** — no gates, since nothing eliminates an option; five weighted criteria. Two majors that pull against each other — *recurring fees and provider coupling*, and *operational burden carried in-house* — plus *availability during event hours* and *update latency* (moderate) and *repeated read load* (minor). I probed for criteria cutting against the pick and kept the one that survived: availability, where a stateful tier you operate yourself is a new event-hours risk and a restart disconnects every browser at once.
- **Options in the Domain** — each described as its advocate would put it, with a fit verdict per criterion. Self-hosted sockets are marked **weak** on operational burden and **partial** on availability; I didn't soften those to match the selection.
- **Selection** — the trade stated plainly: coupling matters more than the fee because it reaches into two nodes, so a later move off a provider is a redesign rather than a swap; the ops burden is accepted, and 800 concurrent connections is what makes it affordable. Polling is recorded as the closer loss and the named fallback if operating the tier proves costlier during event hours than this assumes. Then what it forecloses, and the per-node obligations in the order the nodes get written.

One thing the doc surfaced that I'd rather you settle than leave implicit. Writing out the Selection, the choice turns on no tenet you currently have: *"Self-hosted and managed infrastructure are both permitted"* permits the hosted option, so the preference for owing nothing to a vendor is live judgment that exists only in this one decision. The next choice of the same shape — a managed queue for the publication path, managed Postgres, a metrics vendor — would arrive with nothing to lean on.

Do you want a tenet stating that lean? Something like **"Prefer infrastructure we operate over infrastructure we rent, and pay for it in operations work."** The opposite is a real choice a different project would make — buy the managed thing and spend the saved time on the product — so it passes as a tenet rather than a platitude. If you'd rather keep vendor independence as a one-off call for delivery and judge each future case fresh, that's a legitimate answer too, and I'll leave the tenet list as it stands.

I'm holding the two tenets from before (catch-up completeness, and who suffers under connection pressure) until Phase 2 reaches the node each one bears on — they'll be better questions with that node's design in front of us.
