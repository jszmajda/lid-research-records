Written: `docs/decisions/change-delivery-approach.md`, a project-level decision doc (`node: high-level-design`), with the HLD's Key Design Decisions entry shrunk to a three-line summary plus a pointer to it.

How it's built for a cold reader:

- **Context** states why the question exists from the domain alone — one authoritative board, HTTPS commands, up to 800 browsers per event — and what's at stake: the change store, delivery, and browser sync are all sized by the answer, so a wrong choice means rebuilding three components or running a route that fails at opening time.
- **Decision Elements** names seven things that actually discriminate, each marked major/moderate/minor. The project's standing constraints (one revision per edit, server-side authorization, operational-data-only, the 15-second goal) are stated up front as background rather than gates, because all three options satisfy them — listing them as criteria would have been noise. Latency is framed as *beyond the required floor*, since the floor itself doesn't separate the options. Two criteria are tied to the tenets they derive from.
- **Options in the Domain** gives each of the three a full paragraph in its advocate's voice — including what polling and the hosted service are genuinely good at — then a verdict-led bullet per element. Importance stays on the criterion, fit stays on the option, and no weighing happens in this section.
- **Selection** does the weighing. The three major criteria split the field: latency rules out polling, cost and coupling rule out the hosted service, and operational burden is recorded as the one major criterion arguing against the choice, accepted deliberately rather than discounted. It names *self-hosted and managed infrastructure are both permitted* as the tenet that makes this a weighing rather than a foregone conclusion, and closes with what the choice forecloses and the three downstream obligations.

Two things worth flagging about what the write-up surfaced:

Failure visibility is the criterion self-hosted sockets fit *least* well — an open-but-stalled connection looks exactly like a quiet market, which is precisely what the *connectivity failures must be visible* tenet exists to prevent. Polling scores better there. I recorded this honestly rather than smoothing it, and the Selection converts it into an obligation: browser sync carries an explicit liveness and staleness signal rather than inferring health from the connection being open. That obligation will want its own EARS spec when we reach that node.

The doc's last line leaves open the capacity question I raised before — what the board does when connection capacity is exhausted: protect commit latency by shedding connections, or keep every browser connected and accept slower fan-out. That's the tenet I'd still like your lean on, because it governs choices in all three nodes that no spec will anticipate, and sizing the socket tier runs into it immediately.
