**F1 — High. The job-queue's in-memory queue drops accepted jobs on restart, which runs against the HLD's own "promise" tenet under a condition the docs call routine.** `job-queue-design.md`'s Architecture section states it plainly: "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." That's not a hypothetical edge case — the same doc's Context section names "unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" as something the mini-PC routinely does. So the condition under which jobs vanish isn't rare, it's expected. Weigh that against `high-level-design.md`'s first tenet: "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away." Under *tenet cascade* — the idea that every leaf mechanism should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals point, checked against the operating conditions the docs themselves describe — this is close to the textbook case: the tenet and the mechanism never use the same words, but a restart silently breaks the promise the tenet makes. Why this matters: tenets exist so a future session doesn't have to re-derive which way to lean when something's ambiguous; if a mechanism quietly contradicts a tenet and nobody flags it, the tenet stops being trustworthy as a guide, and the next person extending the queue (e.g., adding retry-on-restart logic, or deciding *not* to) has no record of whether this was already decided or just never noticed.

**F2 — High. The same behavior was never captured as a decision.** There's no row in job-queue's Decisions & Alternatives table for "jobs lost on restart." Compare that to the table's other entries — "Job identifiers: short base32 slugs... accepted cost: slugs recycle after 90 days" is exactly the shape a tradeoff like this should take, and it's missing here. Under *docs carry current intent* (the keep-side of mutation-not-accumulation): a decision that's still live once landed — one a cold reader would question or want to reverse — needs to survive as more than prose buried in an Architecture section. Right now a future reader can't tell whether "in-memory, lost on restart" was a deliberate cost accepted in service of the "mini-PC is modest hardware, service stays small" tenet, or just an oversight nobody connected to the promise tenet. That ambiguity is exactly what compounds: the next person to touch this code has to guess at intent instead of reading it.

**Recommendation**

These two findings are the same underlying gap, so one fix likely closes both. Per the coach's remedy guidance, this doesn't need a full decision doc — it's a single tradeoff, not a researched set of alternatives weighed against criteria. The smallest fix that settles it: add a row to job-queue's Decisions & Alternatives table, something like:

| Decision | Chosen | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Durability of accepted-but-undispatched jobs | In-memory only; lost on restart, member resubmits | Persisting the queue (e.g., to SQLite like the registry) was rejected to keep the service small; accepted cost: breaks the "upload and walk away" promise across the mini-PC's routine unattended restarts |

That's the "record the exception" path from the tenet-conflict remedies — naming the tenet traded away and why — and it closes the decision-capture finding at the same time. Your other options, which I won't pick for you: actually change the design (e.g., persist pending jobs so the promise holds even across a restart), or decide the "promise" tenet itself should carry a caveat for restarts. Since you're still pre-code, this is the cheapest point to make that call — whichever way you lean, I'd write it into the LLD directly rather than going through `/update-lid` (that's for instruction-file/config changes, not design content).
