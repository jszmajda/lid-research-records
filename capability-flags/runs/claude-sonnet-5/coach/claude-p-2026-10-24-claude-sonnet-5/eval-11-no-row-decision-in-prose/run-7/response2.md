The job-queue's accepted-but-undispatched jobs live only in process memory (`job-queue-design.md`'s Architecture section: "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them"). That same doc's Context names the hosting environment as taking "unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" — so the loss condition isn't hypothetical, it's a routine event the doc itself anticipates. Under *tenet cascade* (do the leaf designs lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals point?), this works against two things the HLD states directly: the tenet "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the goal "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." A member who uploads at 9pm, has their job accepted into the queue, and then loses it to a 2am OS update has to notice it's missing and resubmit — exactly the babysitting the service exists to remove, and exactly the overnight window the goal names. The cost of leaving this as-is is that it sits latent until an unattended restart actually happens during a busy queue, and by then it's a support complaint rather than a design conversation.

Separately, under *docs carry current intent* (decision capture — a live choice a cold reader would still question needs its tradeoff reconstructable), this choice has no Decisions & Alternatives row. The same doc captures three other tradeoffs with a rejected alternative and an accepted cost (scheduling order, job identifiers, upload size limit) — but "why in-memory rather than persisted to disk" isn't one of them, even though it's the choice most likely to be questioned given what Context already says about the hardware. Right now a reader has to infer that this was a deliberate simplicity tradeoff rather than an oversight.

**What I'd recommend:** this is a tenet-or-goal conflict (dimension 19), so the fix is your call among three options, not mine to pick:
- **Change the design** — e.g., persist accepted-but-undispatched jobs to disk (you're already content-hashing uploaded G-code to disk, so the job metadata could ride alongside it) so a restart replays the queue instead of dropping it.
- **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet traded against and why (e.g., "in-memory queue chosen over persisted queue: keeps the mini-PC's service small per the hardware tenet; accepted cost: jobs lost on restart, mitigated by low restart frequency"). This also closes the decision-capture finding (F2) on the same choice — one fix, two findings resolved.
- **Revise the tenet** — if "upload and walk away" was never meant to survive a restart, soften it to say so explicitly.

Recording the exception is the smallest fix if the in-memory design is intentional; changing the design is the fix if it isn't. Either way, this is a doc edit you'd make directly in `job-queue-design.md` — no `/update-lid` or `/arrow-maintenance` needed, since it's not a configuration or structural issue.
