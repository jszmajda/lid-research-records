**F1 — High. The in-memory job queue drops accepted jobs that haven't dispatched yet whenever the mini-PC restarts.** `job-queue-design.md`'s Architecture section holds the per-printer-class queue "entirely in process memory," and says plainly: "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The same doc's Context section tells us restarts aren't rare — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Put those two sentences together and you get a real scenario: a member uploads a job at 9pm, an unattended update restarts the box at 2am before that job reaches the front of its class queue, and the job is simply gone — with no notification, since nothing in the design describes telling the member. That lands squarely against two things the HLD commits to: the tenet *"a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away,"* and the goal *"evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present."* This is **tenet cascade** — checking whether a leaf design's mechanisms work against a tenet or goal under an operating condition the docs themselves describe, even though the wording never overlaps (a storage decision vs. a trust-promise tenet, no shared vocabulary). Leaving this in place means the project's own documented restart pattern quietly defeats its headline promise — and because nothing flags the conflict, it'll surface later as a confused member report rather than a design review catching it for free, right when it's cheapest to fix.

**F2 — Medium. That same tradeoff was never written down as a decision.** `job-queue-design.md`'s Decisions & Alternatives table has three rows (scheduling order, job identifiers, upload size) — the restart-loses-jobs behavior isn't one of them. Compare to how the FIFO scheduling decision is captured: chosen option, rejected alternative, accepted cost, all in one row. The persistence choice gets none of that treatment; it's stated as architecture fact with no sign anyone weighed "persist the queue" against "accept the data loss." That's **decision capture** — a decision a cold reader would still question deserves its rejected alternative and accepted cost on the record, the same way the other three rows do. Right now a future session can't tell if this was a deliberate call (maybe: SQLite-backing the queue felt like more complexity than a 6-printer mini-PC warrants) or just never occurred to anyone. Fixing F1 by recording an accepted exception would also close this finding on the same row — they're two angles on one choice, not two separate problems to solve.

**Recommended options for F1 (your call, not mine to pick):**
1. **Change the design** — persist accepted-but-undispatched jobs (e.g., append them to the existing content-hash file store, or a small SQLite table alongside the registry's) and replay them into the in-memory queue on the startup rebuild, next to the existing poll-each-printer step in QUEUE-004.
2. **Record the exception** — if dropping unsent jobs on restart is genuinely acceptable for a 6-printer makerspace queue, add a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet traded away and why (e.g., "restarts are rare enough / resubmitting is a 30-second action" — whatever the real reasoning is), closing F2 at the same time.
3. **Revise the tenet** — if "upload and walk away" was always meant to exclude the restart edge case, narrow its wording so it stops overclaiming.

Since this is still pre-implementation, option 1 is cheapest right now — before any code exists to retrofit. If you want to go that route, `/linked-intent-dev` (describing "persist accepted jobs across restart") would walk the design change forward through the LLD and specs; `/update-lid` alone is for configuration reconciliation, not this kind of content change.
