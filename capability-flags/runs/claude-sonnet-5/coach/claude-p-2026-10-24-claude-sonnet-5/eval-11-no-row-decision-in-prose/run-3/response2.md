**F1 — High. The in-memory queue drops accepted-but-undispatched jobs across a restart, which cuts against the HLD's own promise.** `job-queue-design.md`'s Architecture section says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that on startup the service only rebuilds state by polling printers for their *currently running* job — "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The same doc's Context names the exact condition that triggers this: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." So this isn't a hypothetical edge case — it's a named, expected operating condition. Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — a tenet is a one-line lean for decisions no spec covers — the HLD states "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away." A restart that silently drops a queued job and requires the member to notice and resubmit directly works against that promise: the member walked away, as instructed, and their job vanished without their knowledge. This matters because the tenet is the thing a future session leans on when it hits a decision this design doesn't cover (e.g., "should we ack the upload before or after it's durably queued?") — if the tenet and the actual mechanism already disagree, that lean points the wrong way, and the disagreement will resurface every time someone touches restart/startup behavior, each time looking like a fresh bug rather than a known, accepted trade.

Tied to this is **F3 — the choice itself was never captured.** The in-memory design is stated as architecture fact, but no Decisions & Alternatives row in `job-queue-design.md` names the trade-off — that durability was traded away, against what cost, and why. Recording the exception (below) would close both findings at once, since the remedy for the tenet conflict is also the capture.

**What I'd recommend** — this is a choice between three moves, and I'd leave the pick to you rather than push one:

- **Change the design** so accepted-but-undispatched jobs survive a restart — e.g., persist the accepted queue (even a flat file or the existing SQLite registry) and replay it on startup alongside the printer poll. This actually honors the tenet as written.
- **Record the exception** — add a Decisions & Alternatives row in `job-queue-design.md` naming the tenet traded against: something like "Queue durability across restarts: not implemented. Rejected because it adds persistence machinery the mini-PC doesn't otherwise need; accepted cost: members must notice and resubmit after a restart, which costs the 'upload and walk away' promise for that window." This is the cheapest fix and is consistent with "minimum surface, maximum discipline" if you've decided the complexity isn't worth it — but it's an explicit trade, not a silent one.
- **Revise the tenet** — if walk-away durability was never meant to survive infrastructure hiccups, narrow the tenet's wording (e.g., "...except across an unattended restart, which is rare and visible in the queue view") so it stops over-promising.

Any of the three is legitimate; I'd lean toward "record the exception" first since it's cheap and makes the current behavior defensible, and revisit "change the design" if restarts turn out to be frequent enough in practice that members actually lose work.
