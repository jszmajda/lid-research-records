**F1 — High. The in-memory queue works against the walk-away promise.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` holds the queue "entirely in process memory" and says accepted-but-undispatched jobs "are gone" after a restart and "members resubmit them"; the same file's Context (`:10`) says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Run the scenario the docs themselves describe: a member uploads in the evening and goes home (the whole point of `docs/high-level-design.md:5`), overnight an unattended update reboots the box, the pending job vanishes, and in the morning there's no part and someone has to be present to notice and resubmit. That defeats the goal "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (`docs/high-level-design.md:26`) and the tenet "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (`:20`). QUEUE-004 (`job-queue-specs.md:6`) only captures rebuilding farm state by polling printers for their *current* job — it recovers in-flight work, not the pending queue — so the EARS layer quietly codifies the gap, and no Decisions row names the tenet/goal being traded against. The principle here is *tenets capture forward-looking preference* (tenets pre-narrow the decisions no spec anticipated, so a mechanism leaning against one is exactly the call that should have been caught) and *coherence is adjacency* (each arrow level agrees with its neighbors). Why it matters: the walk-away promise is the product's reason to exist, and if a routine, described operating condition breaks it, the tenet stops being a reliable guide — a future session reading only the LLD would keep extending a volatile queue and never see the conflict, and "upload from home" quietly degrades into "upload and hope." The remedies are three, and the choice is yours (below).

**F2 — High.
