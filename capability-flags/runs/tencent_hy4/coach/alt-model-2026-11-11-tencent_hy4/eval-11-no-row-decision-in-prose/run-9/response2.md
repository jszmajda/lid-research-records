Here's the in-memory queue thread, walked through in detail. Four of the findings touch it, and they're really one story: a single durability decision that shapes the product's core promise but never got recorded as a decision.

**F2 — High. The in-memory design works against two of the HLD's tenets/goals.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue is "a per-printer-class list of accepted jobs held entirely in process memory," and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The same doc's Context (line 10) names the mini-PC's "unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" — so an overnight reboot is a described operating condition, not a hypothetical. Up at `docs/high-level-design.md:20` the first tenet is "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the Goals section (line 26) is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." Under *tenet cascade* — design nodes lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean — the two rarely share wording, which is exactly why this is easy to miss: nothing in the LLD says "this breaks a tenet," yet under the very condition it describes, an evening upload that hadn't dispatched is gone by morning and someone has to be present to resubmit, so the goal goes unmet. Tenets exist to pre-narrow the choices no spec anticipated; here the design leaned toward "the service stays small" (tenet three) at the expense of tenet one, with no record of the trade, and a future session reading the LLD alone sees a coherent little design while the HLD's central promise quietly goes unmet. As for what to do, I'd offer three directions as your choice rather than pick one: (1) change the design so accepted jobs survive a restart (persist the queue the way the registry does), (2) record the exception in a Decisions & Alternatives row naming the tenet/goal traded against and why, or (3) revise the tenet to honestly scope the promise (e.g., "until dispatch, the job is best-effort"). Option (2) also closes F3 below.

**F3 — High. The durability choice is a live decision with no Decisions row.** The in-memory choice appears only in the Architecture prose (`job-queue-design.md:14`); the Decisions & Alternatives table (lines 29–33) captures scheduling order, job identifiers, and the 64 MB limit, but nothing about persistence — and it's otherwise one of the healthier parts of this project, with real alternatives and accepted costs. A cold reader also has `printer-registry-design.md:14` ("a single SQLite file") right beside it and would reasonably ask why the registry survives a restart but the queue doesn't. Under *docs carry current intent* — docs state present intent; git keeps history — the keep-side protects decisions a reader would still question, while the ceiling says a choice that reads as native once landed needs only the ordinary row. Would a cold reader holding the "upload and walk away" tenet still question or be tempted to reverse this one? Yes, so it stays live; with no row, the next session can't tell whether the loss is a deliberate, accepted trade (small hardware; resubmit is cheap because G-code is content-hashed on disk) or an accident frozen in place. That ambiguity compounds in both directions — someone "fixes" it by adding persistence and unknowingly reverses a decision, or leaves a real problem alone assuming it was intentional. The smallest capture that settles this is a fuller Decisions row in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` naming the rejected alternative (persist the queue to disk/SQLite) and the accepted cost (accepted-but-undispatched jobs are lost on restart; members resubmit; cheap because files are content-hashed). Decision docs are rare and reserved for choices that warrant a debate on the record, so a row is right-sized here; if you take remedy (2) above, that same row can double as the tenet-exception record.

**F5 — Medium. The tenets that govern this decision are unordered and can conflict.** The three tenets in `docs/high-level-design.md:18–22` are a bulleted list, and tenet one ("upload and walk away" — durability) pulls against tenet three ("the service stays small") on exactly the persistence question; the job-queue LLD resolved that tension without any declared priority. A couple also read more like goals/values ("A submitted job is a promise") or a recorded decision ("Staff override members... no silent reordering") than one-line leans. Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* — one-line tie-breakers for the choices no spec anticipates, ordered so the higher one wins when two conflict — a tenet's job is to be there on the cold day an unanticipated decision shows up and say which way to lean. An unordered list with two conflicting entries gives no answer, so the agent samples its own preference — plausibly how "stays small" won here by default. Consider ordering the three by precedence and re-casting any value-shaped ones as one-line leans whose opposite is a real alternative a different project could choose; since this is an HLD edit, it should travel through `/linked-intent-dev` so the cascade holds, rather than being a standalone tweak.

**F4 — Medium. The restart/resubmit behavior has no spec to hold it.** `job-queue-design.md:14` describes the loss-and-resubmit consequence, but `job-queue-specs.md` has no line for it — QUEUE-004 covers "rebuild farm state by polling" on startup, not what happens to accepted jobs — and the same file is silent on the "accept only if the class has a registered printer" rule and the staff/member priority + FIFO scheme. Under *effective intent-tree alignment* — spec IDs trace to LLDs, which trace to HLD concepts — behaviors with no spec are unnamed intent that drifts silently because there's nothing to test against; here the very behavior that conflicts with a tenet is also the one the test layer can't see. Whichever durability direction you choose, give the resulting behavior a line in `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-specs.md` plus a test citing it, and walk it through `/linked-intent-dev` so LLD, spec, test, and code stay in cascade. One tie-in worth keeping in view: F1 flagged that all eight specs carry `[x]` ("Implemented: code and tests exist") with no code or tests in the repo — so any spec you touch here should carry an honest `[ ]` until that bottom half actually exists.
