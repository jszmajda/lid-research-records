**F1 — High. The queue holds accepted jobs only in process memory, so an evening upload can disappear before the morning pickup.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them" — while the same doc's Context (`:10`) says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." The HLD makes "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" a tenet (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) and "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" a goal (`:26`). Under the exact condition the design itself describes — an overnight unattended reboot — a job a member submitted and walked away from is silently dropped, so they arrive to no part and must resubmit. That's the *tenet cascade* pattern — do the leaf mechanisms lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say, especially under operating conditions the docs themselves describe? What sharpens it: the same line 14 already keeps uploaded G-code "on disk under a content hash," so durability is clearly affordable here; "the mini-PC is modest hardware; the service stays small" (`:22`) doesn't really force the loss, which makes the choice read as costing more than it buys. Because the HLD's promise is the architecture-level *why* meant to outlive any implementation, a leaf that quietly defeats it leaves future sessions unable to tell a deliberate trade from an unattended defect — QUEUE-004 pins only the startup rebuild-by-polling, not survival of accepted jobs, so the spec layer offers no anchor either way, and one session will "fix" it with persistence while the next defends the small footprint. I'd offer you three ways forward and let you choose rather than pick one — change the design, record the exception, or revise the tenet (below); if you change the design, that's a change to walk through `/linked-intent-dev` so the LLD, specs, tests and code move together.

**F2 — High. That durability trade-off is the one live decision in the job-queue LLD with no Decisions & Alternatives row.** The table at `job-queue-design.md:29–33` has three rows, and each already does the right thing: shortest-job-first "rejected" with an accepted cost, UUIDs "rejected" with a cost, and the 64 MB limit with its source named. The in-memory / lose-jobs-on-restart choice appears only in prose at `:14` — no row naming the rejected alternative (persist the queue) or the accepted cost (members resubmit; a morning pickup can be missed). This is *decision capture* — a choice a cold reader would still question or want to reverse needs its reasoning on the page, in the design doc where a fresh author would put it. A reader holding the HLD's "a submitted job is a promise" in one hand and the in-memory queue in the other would absolutely question this one; with nothing on the page they can't reconstruct what was given up or why, so they either reverse it silently or freeze an accident as intent, and the ambiguity compounds every time the segment is touched. The good news is the fix is small and already in your own conventions: add a fuller Decisions row to `job-queue-design.md` naming the alternative and the cost. A separate decision doc would be more than this needs here — healthy projects have few, and no other design node sizes or schedules itself around this reasoning — so the row is the right-sized capture. Worth noting: the "record the exception" option below also closes this decision-capture finding, so that route settles both F1 and F2 in one row — though you're still free to pick a different remedy for the tenet conflict.
