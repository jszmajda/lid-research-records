**F1 — High. The in-memory queue works against the HLD's own promise tenet.** `job-queue-design.md` §Architecture says jobs accepted but not yet dispatched "are gone, and members resubmit them," and its §Context notes the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" — so the restart is a described operating condition, not a freak event. The HLD tenet "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the goal "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present," both point the other way. Under *tenet cascade* — design nodes should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean — a mechanism that defeats a goal under a condition the docs themselves describe is the exact seam this lens is for, and no Decisions row records the exception. The cost of leaving it: the first thing a member does after a power blip is lose the print they trusted the service with, and the spec set won't even tell a future agent that happened, because QUEUE-004 only covers rebuilding farm state from printers. Three remedies, and this is your call rather than mine: change the design (persist accepted jobs and restore them on startup), record the exception (a Decisions row naming the promise tenet traded against, and why), or revise the tenet to match the intended reality.

The durability trade-off is real and live, but only lives in prose.** The choice is stated once, in `job-queue-design.md` §Architecture, while the Decisions & Alternatives table covers scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload size — not this. The smallest capture is a fuller Decisions row naming the rejected alternative and the accepted cost; if the team wants the full option space on record, a short decision doc is an option rather than the default.
