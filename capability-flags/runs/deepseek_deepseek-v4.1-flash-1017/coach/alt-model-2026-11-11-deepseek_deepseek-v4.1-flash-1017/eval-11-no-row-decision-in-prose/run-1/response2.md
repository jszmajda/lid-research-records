**F1 — High. The in-memory queue defeats the "submitted job is a promise" tenet under a condition your own docs describe.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them", while the same file's Context (line 10) names exactly the condition that triggers it: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip". The HLD's first tenet (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`) promises members they can "upload and walk away". Under *tenet cascade* — the design nodes should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean — this is a real conflict, not a mere cost: an overnight uploader who walks away can lose the job to an unattended reboot and never know. There's no Decisions row recording the exception (that's F2), so a future cold reader inherits a tenet that quietly isn't true. The remedy is yours to choose: change the design (persist the queue — you already accept SQLite for the registry, so the store exists), record the exception in the Decisions table naming the promise tenet traded against and why, or revise the tenet to best-effort-plus-notify. Recording the exception also closes F2; the other two leave the tenet/design mismatch to be handled on its own terms.

**F2 — High. The durability tradeoff is a live decision with no place on the record.** The Decisions & Alternatives table (`job-queue-design.md:29-33`) carefully captures scheduling order, identifiers, and upload size — each with a rejected alternative and accepted cost — but the biggest choice in the file, keeping the only copy of the queue in memory, appears only as a flat statement in Architecture prose. Under *decision capture* — docs carry current intent, and a live decision belongs written down — this is the one row a cold reader is most likely to question or want to reverse, given the promise tenet and the acknowledged power blips; leaving it unrecorded means the choice reads as an accident rather than intent, and the next session may silently "fix" it or preserve it without knowing why. The smallest settling move is a fuller row, e.g. *Decision: queue durability. Chosen: in-memory queue, accepted jobs lost on restart. Rationale: keeps the service small on the mini-PC; rejected SQLite-backed queue — accepted cost: members resubmit after a restart, in tension with the promise tenet.* If the team wants the full option space on record, a short decision doc is an option — but a row is the normal home. I'd suggest resolving F1 and F2 together, since one row can name the rejected alternative, the accepted cost, and the tenet exception.

One related note: whichever way you go, F5 is worth tightening at the same time — the LLD and QUEUE-004 have the queue polling printers directly for restart recovery, while the registry LLD says the registry owns polling.
