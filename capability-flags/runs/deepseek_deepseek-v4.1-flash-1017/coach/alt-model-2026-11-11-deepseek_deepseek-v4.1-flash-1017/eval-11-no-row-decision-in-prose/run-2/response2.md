**F2 — High. Accepted jobs vanish when the mini-PC restarts, which is the exact failure the HLD promised not to have.** `job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue is "held entirely in process memory," and that jobs "accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." That same LLD's Context (`job-queue-design.md:10`) names the operating condition that causes restarts — "unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." Against that, the HLD tenet at `high-level-design.md:20` reads "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the goal at `:26` is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." Under *tenet cascade* — leaf designs lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean, and an exception is recorded when they don't — this is the textbook case: a power blip during an overnight queue loses jobs silently, and "members resubmit them" assumes a member who was told to walk away is watching. The asymmetry is telling, too: the registry already persists to SQLite (`printer-registry-design.md:14`), so the project has a durable store on the same mini-PC and chose not to use it for the queue. This matters because nothing downstream carries the tenet — the specs are silent on restart-loss, so a future session implementing `QUEUE-004` will faithfully build the lossy behavior and none of the intent that should have prevented it. The good news: there is no code yet, so resolving this costs a doc edit now rather than a migration later.

**F3 — Medium. The durability trade itself isn't on the record.** The LLD's Decisions & Alternatives table (`job-queue-design.md:29-33`) covers scheduling order, job IDs, and upload size, but not the choice to hold the queue in memory. Under *decision capture* — live choices are written so a cold reader can see what was given up and why — a reader holding that table and the HLD above it would still question this one, and cannot reconstruct the trade. The likely counterweight is the third tenet, "The mini-PC is modest hardware; the service stays small" (`high-level-design.md:22`); if that's the reason, the row should say so and name the accepted cost (jobs lost on restart; resubmission required). Recording that exception would also close the F2 finding, since both hang on the same unrecorded choice.

For the recommendation, *tenets and goals* say the remedy is yours to choose, not mine — three routes, each with a different cost. **Change the design:** persist the queue, most cheaply by appending accepted jobs to the SQLite file the registry already owns (or a small journal beside it), and on startup reconcile pending jobs against each printer's reported state — this also gives `QUEUE-004` one owner of printer polling rather than the startup queue and the registry each polling separately (the F5 overlap). **Record the exception:** keep in-memory and add a Decisions row naming the "promise" tenet traded against and why, accepting that members can lose an overnight job on a power blip. **Revise the tenet:** weaken "a submitted job is a promise" to "best effort while the service is up," which is honest but gives up the product's central draw.
