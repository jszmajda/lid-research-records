**F2 — High. The in-memory queue works against the HLD's promise tenet.** `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14` holds accepted jobs "entirely in process memory," and on restart "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The HLD tenet at `docs/high-level-design.md:20` says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and the first goal (`:26`) is evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup with no member present; the LLD's own Context (`:10`) names the operating conditions — unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip — under which the loss actually happens. Under *tenet cascade* — a leaf design should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean — this mechanism defeats both the promise and the goal in exactly the condition the docs describe, and no Decisions row records the exception. That matters because the tenet is what a cold future session uses as its tie-breaker: one session reads "job is a promise" and adds durability to half the path, another reads the in-memory implementation and quietly reverses the tenet, and the two sessions disagree without either noticing. The tension is partly internal to the HLD, too — "the mini-PC is modest hardware; the service stays small" (`:22`) points the other way — so this is the user's call among three remedies: change the design (persist accepted jobs — a durable accepted-jobs table has a natural home next to the registry's SQLite), record the exception (a Decisions row naming the promise tenet traded against "stays small," and why resubmission is acceptable), or revise the tenet to best-effort. I'd not pick one for you; the choice is a product judgment about how strong the promise really is.

**F3 — High. The persistence trade-off has no capture.** The Decisions & Alternatives table (`job-queue-design.md:29-33`) records FIFO ordering, job-identifier slugs, and the upload size limit, but nothing about keeping the queue in memory versus persisting it. Under *docs carry current intent* (a doc states current intent written to be read cold) — specifically decision capture — this is a live choice a cold reader would still question: holding the row and the HLD above it, they'd ask "wait, a promised job is lost on a power blip?" and the docs give them no rejected alternative or accepted cost to reconstruct the reasoning. Leaving it uncaptured means the next session can't tell whether job loss is a deliberate accepted cost or an accident of the first implementation, so it either preserves it defensively or reverses it blind. The smallest fix is a fuller row: chosen "in-memory queue," rejected alternative "durable queue (SQLite/file) survive restart," accepted cost "accepted-but-undispatched jobs lost on restart, member resubmits; keeps the mini-PC footprint small." Recording that row also closes the F2 exception if you decide to keep the behavior.

A decision doc is only worth considering if the team wants the full option space on record (sync flush vs. write-ahead vs. keep-in-memory with resubmission prompts, weighed); a table row is the right default here.
