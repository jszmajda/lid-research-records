**F1 — High. The in-memory queue works against the HLD's own promise, and no exception is recorded.** `job-queue-design.md:14` says the queue is held entirely in process memory and that "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched before a restart are gone, and members resubmit them." The HLD's Context (`high-level-design.md:10`) already names the operating condition that triggers this — the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" — so lost jobs aren't a rare edge case, they're a designed-for event. That collides head-on with the tenet at `high-level-design.md:20` ("A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away") and the goal at line 26 ("Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present"). Under *tenet cascade* — the design should lean the way the HLD's tenets and goals say to lean, or say out loud why it doesn't — this is the pattern worth catching: a member uploads in the evening, a scheduled update reboots the box overnight, and the morning pickup silently doesn't happen. Leaving it unrecorded means every future session reads the tenet and the design as if they agree, and one of them quietly wins by default. For the remedy, the three options are yours to weigh: change the design (persist accepted jobs), record the exception (a Decisions row naming the promise/durability tenet being traded away and why), or revise the tenet so it no longer promises more than the service delivers.

**F2 — High. The persistence choice is live but uncaptured.** The job-queue Decisions & Alternatives table covers scheduling order, job identifiers, and upload size limit, but not the choice to keep the queue only in memory. This is exactly the kind of decision *decision capture* — docs carry current intent, including what was given up — is meant to preserve: a cold reader holding the design and the HLD would question it and might reverse it, so the row should let them reconstruct the tradeoff. Concretely, `printer-registry` already persists to a single SQLite file (`printer-registry-design.md:14`), so durable storage is not a new dependency or a hardware risk — a reader can't tell whether in-memory was a deliberate simplicity bet or an accident that survived. The smallest fix that settles it is a fuller row naming the rejected alternative (persisting accepted jobs to that SQLite file, or a small journal) and the accepted cost (durability sacrificed on restart, members must resubmit). If the team wants the full option space on record, a decision doc is an option rather than a recommendation — and note that recording the exception here also closes F1's tenet conflict, so one row can resolve both findings.
