**F1: The in-memory queue conflicts with the "promise" tenet and the overnight-print goal.** The HLD's first tenet says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away." Its first goal is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." The job-queue design keeps the queue "entirely in process memory," so a restart drops every accepted-but-undispatched job, and members have to resubmit. The design names the restart triggers itself: the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." So a member who uploads in the evening and walks away can come back to a queue that silently lost the job. That is the tenet-cascade lens at work: the mechanism works against a tenet and a goal under a condition the design describes, and the two share no wording, which is why it's easy to miss. Nothing on the page records that this was traded on purpose. Left alone, the gap will surface as a lost print the first time a power blip hits, and by then the design choice has been in use for months. You could resolve it three ways, and the choice is yours:

- **Change the design so accepted jobs survive a restart.** The registry already persists to a single SQLite file, so a jobs table in that file is one option. It costs some of the "modest hardware, small service" tenet, but less than adding a new component would.
- **Keep the in-memory queue and record the exception.** Add a Decisions row that names the promise tenet and the overnight goal, states the restart loss as the accepted cost, and says what members are told to do about it.
- **Revise the tenet.** If "promise" was never meant to cover restarts, say so in the HLD and narrow it.

**F5: The in-memory choice has no Decisions row.** This is the decision-capture side of the same choice. A cold reader of the job-queue design sees a Decisions table with three rows (scheduling order, job identifiers, upload size) and no row for where the queue lives. The cost is described in the Architecture prose, but a reader has to hunt for it. A cold reader would also ask the obvious question: why is the registry persisted to SQLite while the queue is not? The registry row's rationale is explicitly about the registry alone ("nothing else needs concurrent writers"), so it doesn't answer that. The uploaded G-code files are kept on disk under a content hash, so they survive restarts, while the job records that point at them do not. That split is worth naming in the record. The smallest fix is a fuller row for queue storage: the chosen option, the rejected alternative, and the accepted cost. Whichever option you pick for F1, that row is what settles F5, so the two findings close together.

**What I'd do first:** decide F1 before touching anything else. It's the one with a user-facing consequence, and the answer determines the wording of the F5 row. If you choose to keep the in-memory queue, F5 is a short edit to `docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md`. If you choose persistence, the change lands in the job-queue LLD and needs a new spec for restart recovery, which doesn't exist yet. I haven't changed any files; this review is advisory.
