**F1 — High. Accepted jobs live only in memory, so a restart loses them.** The job-queue design says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that jobs accepted but not yet dispatched "are gone, and members resubmit them" (`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md:14`). The Context section says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip" (`job-queue-design.md:10`), so restarts happen overnight, when no member is there to notice. That runs against two things the HLD commits to. The first is the tenet "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away" (`docs/high-level-design.md:20`). The second is the goal "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present" (`high-level-design.md:26`). Under *tenets capture forward-looking preference* (a tenet is a tie-breaker the team leans toward when a design choice is open), the tenet is meant to decide this kind of question, and here it doesn't. The design has also not recorded the trade. The Decisions table in the job-queue LLD has rows for scheduling order, job IDs, and upload size, but none for in-memory storage, so the cost (lost jobs on restart) is written only in the Architecture prose. The doc is also inconsistent with itself: uploaded G-code files are kept on disk under a content hash, and the registry persists printers in SQLite, but queued jobs are not persisted at all. QUEUE-004 rebuilds farm state by polling printers, which can recover what is running but not what is waiting.

Why this matters: the restart is the moment when the promise breaks. Every power blip or update during the night loses jobs that members believed were queued, and the member finds out in the morning, with no way to know which jobs disappeared. Under *docs carry current intent*, a future session that reads the tenet and then the Architecture will find a contradiction and have to guess which one holds. The design decision is not wrong in itself, but it is currently made silently, and it is the sort of choice a cold reader will question.

There are three ways to settle it, and the choice is yours:

1. **Change the design: persist accepted jobs.** This is the option I'd lean toward. The registry already uses a single SQLite file, and the HLD says the mini-PC should stay small. A jobs table in SQLite adds little code, and it lets QUEUE-004 recover waiting jobs as well as running ones. It also means the "small service" tenet and the "promise" tenet can both hold. The change would touch the Architecture section of the job-queue LLD, QUEUE-004, and possibly a new spec for "accepted jobs survive a restart." Those edits belong in a design change, so I'd run `/linked-intent-dev` with a description of the change instead of editing by hand.
2. **Keep memory-only and record the exception.** Add a Decisions row to the job-queue LLD that names the tenet traded against (the promise tenet) and the reason (the mini-PC is modest, and the team accepts lost jobs on restart). Then tell members in the UI that the queue does not survive a restart. This is honest, but it weakens the goal "no member present," and it leaves the tenet and the design in visible tension.
3. **Revise the tenet.** If "a submitted job is a promise" was meant loosely, narrow it to what the design actually does, for example "a submitted job is kept until dispatch, unless the service restarts." This is the cheapest edit, but it changes a stated goal, so it should be your call.
