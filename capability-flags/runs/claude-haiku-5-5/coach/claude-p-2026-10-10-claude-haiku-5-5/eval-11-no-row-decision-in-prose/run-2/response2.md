**F1: Accepted jobs are lost on restart, which works against the project's own promise.** The job-queue LLD keeps the queue "entirely in process memory" and says that jobs accepted but not yet dispatched are "gone" after a restart, so members must resubmit. The same LLD's Context says the mini-PC "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip," so restarts are a normal operating condition, not an edge case. The HLD's first tenet says "a submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and its first goal is "evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup, with no member present." Under *tenet cascade*, which asks whether a mechanism works against a tenet or goal under a condition the docs describe, an evening upload followed by an overnight OS update leaves the member with no job and no warning. The docs describe the condition and the mechanism, but nothing records that the tenet was traded against. This matters because the failure is silent: the member finds out in the morning, which is the moment the goal says should be the pickup. Three remedies are open, and the choice is yours. You could change the design, for example by persisting accepted jobs. The registry already uses a SQLite file, so the queue could share it, though that would add a writer and cut against tenet 3 (keep the service small). You could record the exception: add a Decisions row that names the tenet traded against and why, so the cost is on the record. Or you could revise the tenet, for example by rewording it to "accepted jobs survive planned restarts" if the design is meant to tolerate unplanned loss.
