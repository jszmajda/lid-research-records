Overkill. The Decisions & Alternatives row in the registry LLD is enough.

The test is whether a cold reader of the finished design would question the choice or be tempted to reverse it, not whether it was hard to decide. Here the HLD tenets (no runtime network access, USB snapshots as the only update channel) rule out the HTTPS directory and the managed search service. A reader who sees SQLite next to those tenets won't wonder why. The row already names both rejected alternatives and the tenet that excludes them, so it settles the question in one line.

Your back-and-forth was real, but it came from the options looking close on their own merits, before the tenets were applied. Once they were applied, one option dominated. A decision doc earns its place only when a reader would otherwise re-litigate the choice without the competing options and criteria in front of them.

The one case where this would change is if the no-network tenets were ever relaxed, for example by allowing an on-site directory. The choice would then be live again, and a decision doc would make sense at that point.

If you'd still like one for your own records, I can write it, but I'd keep the row as the canonical record.
