**F1 (high). The job-queue keeps accepted jobs only in memory, and that works against the HLD's first tenet and first goal.**

`docs/intent/job-queue/job-queue-design.md` (Architecture) says the queue is "held entirely in process memory" and that after a restart, "jobs that were accepted but not yet dispatched… are gone, and members resubmit them." The same LLD's Context says the service runs on a mini-PC that "takes unattended OS updates and the occasional power blip." The HLD says "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away," and its first goal is "Evening upload, overnight print, morning pickup — with no member present." The docs never use the same words for these, but the conditions line up. Jobs are uploaded in the evening and sit queued until printers free up overnight, which is when unattended updates and blips happen. A restart in that window silently drops a member's job, and the member finds out in the morning when nothing is in the tray. This is *tenet cascade*: the design nodes should lean the way the HLD's tenets say to lean, and a mechanism that defeats a tenet under a condition the docs themselves describe needs to be either changed or explicitly excepted. The cost of leaving it is that the promise tenet is not true of the system as designed, and a future session reading the HLD will assume it is.

**F2 (high). The choice has no Decisions row.** The in-memory queue appears only as a statement in Architecture prose. The Decisions & Alternatives table covers scheduling order, job IDs and upload size, but not queue durability. A cold reader cannot tell whether this was a deliberate trade for the "service stays small" tenet or an oversight. A reader would plausibly want to reverse it, so it is a live decision, and it fails the test of whether they can reconstruct what was given up and what cost was accepted. This is the *docs carry current intent* keep-side applied to decisions: a choice that stays live belongs in the table, with its accepted cost written down.

**What I'd recommend.** Which way to resolve F1 is your call, and there are three reasonable options:

1. **Change the design.** Persist accepted jobs so they survive a restart. The registry already uses a single SQLite file, and the job-queue could reuse that pattern (or add a table to it). That restores the promise tenet, and `QUEUE-004` would need a companion spec such as "when the service starts, the system SHALL restore accepted jobs from the store". It also needs a new Decisions row for the store.
2. **Record the exception.** Keep memory-only, and add a Decisions row naming the tenet traded against ("a submitted job is a promise"), why ("service stays small", no durable store for a queue of at most a few dozen jobs), and the accepted cost (jobs not yet dispatched are lost on restart). If you go this way, consider also making the loss visible to members, for example by notifying on restart which jobs were dropped, so "walk away" degrades into something honest.
3. **Revise the tenet.** If losing jobs on restart is acceptable, soften the HLD tenet and the overnight goal so they don't promise more than the design delivers.

Recording the exception (option 2) also closes F2. Options 1 and 3 should still get a Decisions row, since each is a live choice.

Both the Decisions row and, for option 1, the new spec and LLD edits are changes to your docs, so you'd make them, and I can help draft the row wording if you tell me which direction you're leaning. Since the tenets are unordered, this is also a good moment to decide which of "promise" and "stay small" wins when they conflict (F3). That answer would settle this case and future ones like it.
