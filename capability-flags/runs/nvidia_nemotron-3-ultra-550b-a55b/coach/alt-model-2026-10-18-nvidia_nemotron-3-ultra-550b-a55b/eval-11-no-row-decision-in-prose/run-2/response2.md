**F1 — Medium. The job-queue LLD keeps accepted jobs in process memory; on restart, undispatched jobs are gone and members must resubmit.** This is documented explicitly in the LLD's Architecture section and the Decisions table (the "Scheduling order" row accepts the cost that "one long job can hold a class for hours"), but the design does not name the tenet it trades against. HLD Tenet 1 says: "A submitted job is a promise: members should be able to upload and walk away." *Tenet cascade* — design nodes should lean the way HLD tenets say to lean; when they don't, record the exception naming the tenet and why. The conflict is subtle: the queue rebuilds farm state by polling printers on startup (QUEUE-004), so dispatched jobs are not lost, but *accepted-but-not-yet-dispatched* jobs vanish — a member who uploads at 10 PM for an overnight print on a busy class could wake up to find their job gone. That breaks the "walk away" promise for a real operating condition the docs describe (evening upload, overnight print, popular class). The LLD already carries the decision and its accepted cost; what's missing is the explicit linkage to the tenet. 

**Recommended action:** Add a row to the job-queue LLD's Decisions & Alternatives table (or a Decision doc if you want the full option space on record) that names Tenet 1, describes the condition (restart while jobs wait in a busy class), and records why the trade was accepted — e.g., "Mini-PC takes unattended OS updates; persistence adds complexity and disk wear; accepted cost: members on busy classes may lose queued jobs on restart, mitigated by staff reordering and the short expected uptime between updates." This closes the tenet-cascade finding and, if the same choice also had no decision record, the decision-capture finding — both in one row.
